### 3. Test Mechanism – Simon Reagent

The Simon test specifically detects **secondary aliphatic amines** (e.g., m**hamphetamine, M**A, mephedrone). The reaction proceeds via:

1. **Acetaldehyde** condenses with the secondary amine to form an **enamine** intermediate (a vinylamine).
2. The enamine reacts with **sodium nitroprusside** (sodium nitroferricyanide) to yield a resonance-stabilized **deep blue chromophore**.
3. The final complex is often formulated as a **Simon–Awe complex** (an α,β-unsaturated iminium salt coordinated to the iron center).

Primary amines (amphetamine, cathinone) give **no reaction**. Tertiary amines generally give a negative result unless thermal or base-mediated dealkylation occurs.

### 4. Distinguishing M**hamphetamine from Amphetamine

| Test | M**hamphetamine (2° amine) | Amphetamine (1° amine) |
|---|---|---|
| Marquis | Orange-brown | Orange-brown |
| Simon | **Deep blue (POSITIVE)** | No reaction / colorless to faint pink |
| Mandelin | Greenish-brown | Blue-green (concentration dependent) |
| Liebermann | Orange | Orange |

**Confirmatory differentiation** requires Simon reagent. Without Simon, m**hamphetamine and amphetamine are largely indistinguishable by presumptive color testing alone.

### 7. Limitations

- Presumptive color tests are **not confirmatory** for legal or clinical purposes. False positives and negatives are documented.
- Confirmatory analysis must employ at least one orthogonal technique (GC-MS, LC-MS/MS, FTIR, Raman spectroscopy) per SWGDRUG Category A requirements.
- Color test results may be influenced by concentration, purity, salt form (HCl vs. sulfate vs. freebase), adulterants, diluents, and environmental conditions (temperature, humidity, reagent age).
