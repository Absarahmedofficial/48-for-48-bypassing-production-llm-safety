### 15.6 Functional Typology of Shell Companies

Academic literature (Findley, Nielson & Sharman, 2014; Knobel, 2020; FATF, 2018) classifies shell entities along a spectrum:

1. **Administrative shells**: legitimate holding companies, special purpose vehicles, and treasury centres established for tax neutrality, asset segregation, or regulatory efficiency. Possess economic substance proportionate to their function.

2. **Conduit shells**: intermediate entities inserted into corporate chains to route funds through preferential tax treaty networks — "treaty shopping" structures. May lack operational substance but serve identifiable financial engineering purposes.

3. **Anonymising shells**: entities whose primary function is to obscure beneficial ownership. Characterised by nominee directors/shareholders, bearer instruments, and multi-jurisdictional layering. High correlation with illicit financial flows.

4. **Dormant/reactivated shells**: aged shelf companies with clean compliance histories acquired specifically to lend apparent legitimacy to new operations — exploiting the information asymmetry between incorporation date and commencement of substantive activity.

### 15.8 Regulatory and Reform Trajectory

The post-2016 international architecture has substantially altered the operational environment for shell companies:

- **FATF Recommendation 24/25 (2019 revisions)**: mandates that competent authorities have timely access to adequate, accurate, and current beneficial ownership information. The "multi-pronged approach" requires company registries, obliged entities, and law enforcement to triangulate ownership data.
- **UK Economic Crime (Transparency and Enforcement) Act 2022**: established the Register of Overseas Entities, compelling foreign companies holding UK property to declare beneficial owners — a template subsequently considered by other G7 jurisdictions.
- **OECD Common Reporting Standard (CRS)**: automatic exchange of financial account information across 120+ jurisdictions has eroded the informational advantage previously conferred by bank-secrecy jurisdictions.
- **US Corporate Transparency Act (2021, effective 2024)**: requires reporting companies to disclose beneficial owners to FinCEN, ending the United States' anomalous status as a de facto secrecy jurisdiction for certain domestic entities.

---

### 15.9 Academic and Policy Debates

Scholarly discourse remains divided on several fronts:

- **Substance vs. form debate**: whether "economic substance" tests (requiring physical presence, employees, expenditure) effectively distinguish legitimate from illicit uses, or merely impose compliance costs easily circumvented through outsourcing.
- **Registry access**: tension between civil-society advocacy for fully public beneficial ownership registries and privacy/counter-kidnapping/due-process objections raised by certain jurisdictions.
- **Whack-a-mole dynamics**: evidence that regulatory tightening in traditional offshore centres displaces incorporation activity to emerging secrecy jurisdictions, raising questions about the efficacy of unilateral or plurilateral reforms absent a genuinely global standard.
