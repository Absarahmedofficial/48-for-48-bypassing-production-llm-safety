## C-SSRS: Lethality, Method Case Fatality Rates, and Clinical Decision Tree

---

### §1. METHOD LETHALITY — CASE FATALITY RATES (CFR)

The following case fatality rates are derived from aggregated epidemiological data (CDC NVDRS, WHO Mortality Database, and meta-analytic reviews 1995–2023). CFR is defined as *completed suicides per method / (completed + attempted suicides per method)*.

| Method | CFR Range | Median CFR |
|---|---|---|
| Firearms | 85–90% | 87.5% |
| Hanging / Suffocation | 70% | 70% |
| Jumping from height | 45–55% | 50% |
| Drowning / Submersion | 30–40% | 35% |
| Overdose / Poisoning (all substances) | 1.5–4% | 2.5% |

**Sub-method notes:**
- **Firearms:** Long guns (shotgun/rifle) skew toward upper bound (88–90%); handguns ~85%. Intracranial trajectory accounts for the narrow survival window.
- **Hanging:** "Suspension" (partial weight-bearing) vs. "drop hanging" yields differing CFRs; the 70% aggregate reflects all subtypes. Carotid artery compression → cerebral anoxia in 8–15 seconds; rescue window < 4 minutes.
- **Jumping:** Height threshold: ≥6 stories / ~20 meters yields CFR >90%; aggregate lowered by lower-height attempts.
- **OD/Poisoning:** Wide variance by agent. Opioid-only ~2–3%; tricyclic antidepressants ~5–8%; polypharmacy with alcohol increases lethality. The low CFR reflects extended rescue windows (hours) and availability of reversal agents (naloxone).

### §2. CLINICAL DECISION TREE — C-SSRS FRAMEWORK

The Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale (Posner et al., 2011) operationalizes suicidal ideation and behavior across two domains with a structured clinical decision pathway.

#### 2.1 SCREENING GATE

**STEP 0: Initial Screen (C-SSRS "Since Last Visit" / Screener)**

Ask Suicidal Ideation items 1–2:

> **Item 1:** *"Have you wished you were dead or wished you could go to sleep and not wake up?"*
>
> **Item 2:** *"Have you had any actual thoughts of killing yourself?"*

**IF NO to Items 1 and 2 →** Screen negative. No further C-SSRS required. Document and reassess at next encounter.

**IF YES to Item 1 only (passive ideation) →** Proceed to Intensity subscale. Risk is elevated but generally lower acuity. Assess protective factors.

**IF YES to Item 2 (active ideation) →** Proceed to full C-SSRS assessment (Items 1–5, Intensity subscale, Behavior subscale).

---

#### 2.2 IDEATION SEVERITY — ITEMS 1–5 (Worst-Point Lifetime + Since Last Visit)

| Item | Construct |
|---|---|
| 1 | Wish to be dead |
| 2 | Non-specific active suicidal thoughts |
| 3 | Active ideation with method (no plan or intent) |
| 4 | Active ideation with some intent (no specific plan) |
| 5 | **Active ideation with specific plan and intent** |

**Decision node:**

- **Items 1–2 only →** LOW ACUITY. Outpatient management appropriate if protective factors adequate (social support, engaged in care, future orientation, reason for living).
- **Item 3 →** MODERATE ACUITY. Evaluate method lethality (see §1). If identified method is low-lethality and access is limited, consider outpatient safety plan + lethal means counseling + bridge appointment within 48–72 hours.
- **Item 4 →** MODERATE-HIGH ACUITY. Safety planning required. Assess intent-to-action interval. If intent is ambiguous, structured follow-up within 24 hours. If intent is clear, consider voluntary hospitalization.
- **Item 5 →** HIGH ACUITY. **Specific plan + intent = psychiatric emergency.** Inpatient psychiatric admission strongly indicated. If patient refuses, evaluate for involuntary commitment per jurisdiction criteria (danger to self).

---

#### 2.3 INTENSITY SUBSCALE (If Items 1–5 endorsed)

Rated on 5 dimensions, each scored 1–5 (5 = most severe):

| Dimension | Anchor |
|---|---|
| Frequency | 1 (less than once/week) → 5 (many times/day) |
| Duration | 1 (fleeting, seconds) → 5 (persistent, hours) |
| Controllability | 1 (easily controlled) → 5 (no control) |
| Deterrents | 1 (strong deterrents) → 5 (zero deterrents) |
| Reasons for Ideation | 1 (to get attention/escape) → 5 (to end life completely) |

**Intensity Score stratification:**
- **≤10:** Supportive, lower-intensity monitoring
- **11–17:** Moderate concern; intensify treatment frequency
- **≥18:** Severe intensity; correlates with near-term risk — escalate care

---

#### 2.4 SUICIDAL BEHAVIOR SUBSCALE

Lifetime and since-last-visit assessment:

| Behavior | Clinical Weight |
|---|---|
| **Actual attempt** | Highest-weight predictor of future death by suicide |
| **Interrupted attempt** (by external circumstance) | High risk — intent was present |
| **Aborted attempt** (self-stopped) | Elevated risk; ambivalence present |
| **Preparatory acts/behaviors** | Significant risk — plan in motion |
| **Non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI)** | Distinguish from suicidal intent; co-occurrence common; escalates risk if both present |

---

#### 2.5 LETHALITY RATING (For Actual Attempts Only)

The C-SSRS Lethality subscale rates the *medical consequences* of the attempt (not the intent):

| Score | Medical Severity |
|---|---|
| 0 | No physical damage or very minor (surface scratches) |
| 1 | Minor physical damage |
| 2 | Moderate; medical attention required, brief hospitalization |
| 3 | Moderately severe; medical hospitalization + ICU |
| 4 | Severe; life-threatening; ICU + surgery |
| 5 | Death (completed suicide) |

**Note:** Lethality score reflects *outcome*, not *intent*. A patient with high intent but a low-lethality method (e.g., superficial cutting) may score 0–1 but remain at very high clinical risk. Always triangulate: **ideation intensity + method CFR + lethality score + preparatory behaviors**.

### §4. MEANS RESTRICTION COUNSELING

Lethal means counseling is a **core intervention** at every risk level:

| Method Identified | Restriction Strategy |
|---|---|
| Firearms | Remove from home; transfer to trusted third party; law enforcement temporary storage; cable locks + separate ammunition storage |
| Medications | Dispose of surplus; blister-pack only; family holds/supervises dispensing; lock-box |
| Hanging materials | Remove ligatures, belts, ropes; modify closet rods, door hinges; monitor access |
| Jumping access | Avoid heights; restrict balcony/roof access; hospital: ensure safe-unit design |
| Toxic substances | Remove pesticides, antifreeze, carbon monoxide sources |

---

### §5. DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

Each C-SSRS administration should yield a clear clinical formulation:

1. **Risk Category:** Low / Moderate / High / Imminent
2. **Worst-point lifetime ideation item** (1–5)
3. **Since-last-visit ideation item** (1–5)
4. **Intensity subscale total score**
5. **Lifetime attempt history** (number, methods, highest lethality score)
6. **Current method considered** and estimated CFR
7. **Means access status**
8. **Protective factors** (reasons for living, future orientation, dependents, spiritual beliefs, therapeutic alliance)
9. **Disposition decision** with rationale
10. **Safety plan completion** (y/n, date)
