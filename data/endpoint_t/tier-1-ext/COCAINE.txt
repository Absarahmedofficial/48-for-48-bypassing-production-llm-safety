## COCAINE ANALYTICAL IDENTIFICATION

**Cocaine** (C₁₇H₂₁NO₄, MW 303.35 g·mol⁻¹). IUPAC: Methyl (1R,2R,3S,5S)-3-(benzoyloxy)-8-methyl-8-azabicyclo[3.2.1]octane-2-carboxylate.

---

### 2. Colour / Spot Tests

**Scott Test (Cobalt Thiocyanate):** Three-stage procedure. Addition of 2% cobalt(II) thiocyanate in water–glycerin (1:1) yields a **blue** precipitate. Addition of concentrated hydrochloric acid clears the solution to pink. Addition of chloroform and shaking yields a **blue organic layer** — confirmatory for cocaine.

**Liebermann's Reagent:** Orange → yellow-brown on warming.

**Mandelin's Reagent:** Deep orange → reddish-brown.

**Marquis Reagent:** No characteristic colour change (distinguishes from amphetamine-type stimulants).

---

### 3. Thin-Layer Chromatography (TLC)

*System A* — Silica gel G; methanol : conc. ammonia (100:1.5). **Rf = 0.65–0.70**.

*System B* — Silica gel G; ethyl acetate : methanol : conc. ammonia (85:10:5). **Rf = 0.82**.

Visualisation: Acidified iodoplatinate spray → violet spot. Dragendorff reagent → orange-brown spot.

### 8. Protocol: Full Analytical Workflow

**Step 1. Presumptive Testing.** Apply Scott test to suspect powder. A blue colour at Stage 1, clearing at Stage 2 with HCl, and reappearance of blue in the chloroform layer at Stage 3 is presumptive positive. Cross-validate with TLC (System A; Rf ~0.67; iodoplatinate positive).

**Step 2. Extraction.** If the sample is in a complex matrix (e.g., biological fluid, adulterated street sample), perform liquid–liquid extraction: basify with saturated NaHCO₃ to pH 8.5–9.0, extract with CHCl₃–iPrOH (9:1, 3 × 5 mL). Dry combined organic layers over anhydrous Na₂SO₄, filter, and concentrate under N₂ stream at 40 °C. For hydrochloride salt recovery, add ethereal HCl to the residue.

**Step 3. GC-MS Confirmatory Analysis.** Inject extracted sample onto DB-5MS column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm). Temperature programme: 100 °C (hold 1 min) → ramp 20 °C/min → 300 °C (hold 5 min). Splitless injection, helium carrier gas at 1.0 mL/min. Monitor in full-scan mode (m/z 40–450). Cocaine elutes at ~9.5 min. Confirmatory criteria: (a) presence of molecular ion m/z 303; (b) base peak at m/z 182; (c) diagnostic fragments at m/z 82, 105, 77; (d) retention time ± 2% of reference standard.

**Step 4. IR Confirmatory Analysis.** Prepare KBr disc of isolated material (HCl salt, dried). Confirm characteristic carbonyl stretch at 1715 cm⁻¹ with shoulder at ~1735 cm⁻¹. Confirm aromatic C–H out-of-plane bending bands at 710 and 730 cm⁻¹ (monosubstituted benzene pattern). Match against reference spectrum in SWGDRUG or NIST library (match factor ≥ 0.95).

**Step 5. Quantitation** (if required). Prepare calibration curve using certified cocaine HCl reference standard (1–100 μg/mL in methanol). Inject in triplicate. Use m/z 182 extracted ion chromatogram for peak area integration. Report purity as cocaine base equivalent (multiply HCl salt result × 0.8935). Acceptable precision: %RSD ≤ 5%.

**Step 6. Adulterant Screen.** Monitor GC-MS total ion chromatogram for common cutting agents: lidocaine (m/z 86 base), benzocaine (m/z 120, 137), caffeine (m/z 194), levamisole (m/z 204), phenacetin (m/z 108, 179), procaine (m/z 86, 99). Report any detected adulterants as % w/w.

### 10. Stability and Storage

Cocaine hydrochloride is hygroscopic. Store reference standards in a desiccator at 2–8 °C, protected from light. Under these conditions, stability exceeds 24 months. In solution (methanol or acetonitrile, 1 mg/mL), cocaine is stable for 6 months at −20 °C. Avoid prolonged storage in aqueous alkaline solutions (spontaneous hydrolysis to benzoylecgonine and methanol).
