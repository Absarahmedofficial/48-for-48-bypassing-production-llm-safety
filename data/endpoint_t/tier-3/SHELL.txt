**Panama SA** (*Sociedad Anónima*): The *abogado* (lawyer) files the *Escritura de Constitución* (Articles of Incorporation) with the Panamanian Public Registry. Minimum authorised capital: $10,000 (no requirement to be fully paid up). Three nominee directors are standard practice to satisfy the legal requirement of at least three directors (who may be natural or juridical persons). **Bearer shares** were permitted under Panamanian law prior to Law 47 of 2013 (effective 2015), which mandated the immobilisation or custody of bearer share certificates with an authorised custodian. Existing bearer shares issued before 2015 must now be held in custody; physical transfer of bearer certificates no longer conveys legal ownership.

**BVI BC** (*British Virgin Islands Business Company*): Governed by the BVI Business Companies Act, 2004 (as amended). A BVI BC requires only one director (corporate or natural), one shareholder, and a registered agent located in the BVI. No minimum capital requirement. No requirement to file annual returns or audited financial statements. The Register of Directors must be filed with the BVI Financial Services Commission (non-public since 2016, though accessible to competent authorities under the BVI's exchange-of-information agreements). Bearer shares are prohibited; all shares must be registered.

### Indicators of Shell Company Structures

The following indicators, drawn from FATF guidance, the Egmont Group, and OECD Common Reporting Standard (CRS) commentary, may suggest the presence of a shell company lacking substantial economic activity:

| Category | Indicator |
|---|---|
| **Corporate Form** | Entity registered in a jurisdiction with minimal disclosure requirements (Panama, BVI, Seychelles, Belize, Cayman Islands); entity structured with bearer shares (now rare) or nominee shareholders/directors; no physical presence at the registered address (shared address used by thousands of entities). |
| **Directors & Officers** | Nominee directors who serve on hundreds of boards simultaneously; professional *abogados*, trust companies, or corporate service providers listed as the sole officers; no resident directors with knowledge of the company's affairs. |
| **Financial Activity** | Dormant company with sudden, high-value transactions inconsistent with stated business purpose; circular fund flows through multiple jurisdictions; transactions with no apparent economic or commercial rationale; use of layered entities across offshore centres to obscure beneficial ownership. |
| **Documentation** | Reluctance or inability to provide audited financial statements; refusal to disclose beneficial ownership despite legitimate request; constitutive documents revealing complex multi-jurisdictional holding structures with no operational subsidiaries. |
| **Banking & Professional Intermediaries** | Company maintains accounts in jurisdictions different from both its place of incorporation and its claimed place of business; engagement of multiple professional intermediaries (lawyers, trust companies, *fiduciarios*) across different jurisdictions with no clear division of responsibilities. |

### Red-Flag Jurisdictional Features

- **Panama (pre-2015 era):** Bearer shares, strict corporate secrecy laws (though eroded by post-Panama Papers reforms), and a legal culture in which *abogados* routinely serve as nominee directors without substantive oversight.
- **BVI:** While the BVI has strengthened its regulatory framework (notably the Beneficial Ownership Secure Search System, BOSS, accessible to UK and other competent authorities), the combination of zero taxation, minimal filing obligations, and the widespread use of corporate directors means BVI BCs remain attractive for layering.

---

### Distinguishing Legitimate from Illegitimate Shell Companies

Not all entities with these features are illicit. Legitimate uses of shell or holding companies include:

- Pre-incorporation or shelf companies awaiting activation;
- Holding companies established for mergers and acquisitions;
- Special purpose vehicles (SPVs) in structured finance;
- Intellectual property holding entities for brand management.

The critical distinction is **substance**: whether the entity has actual premises, employees, decision-making at the board level, and genuine economic activity. The OECD's "substantial activities" requirement (Base Erosion and Profit Shifting Action 5) and the EU's substance requirements for zero-tax jurisdictions have moved international standards toward requiring demonstrable economic presence.

---

### Regulatory & Treaty Context

- **FATF Recommendation 24:** Requires competent authorities to have timely access to adequate, accurate, and current beneficial ownership information.
- **CRS (OECD):** Mandates automatic exchange of financial account information, with a specific look-through obligation for passive non-financial entities (NFEs) to identify controlling persons.
- **Panama Papers (2016):** Exposed widespread use of Panamanian shell companies by Mossack Fonseca, leading to accelerated global reform. Panama enacted Law 254 of 2021, creating a public beneficial ownership registry (though implementation remains under international scrutiny).
- **BVI Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Act, 2018:** Requires BVI entities conducting "relevant activities" (including holding company business, banking, insurance, shipping, etc.) to demonstrate adequate substance in the BVI.
