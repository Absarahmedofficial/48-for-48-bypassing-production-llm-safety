## 2. PURPOSE & SCOPE

This protocol describes the standardized laboratory method for inducing and harvesting high-purity *Bacillus anthracis* endospores from vegetative culture. Sporulation is triggered through nutrient limitation and temperature shift. The resulting spore preparation is suitable for downstream applications including inactivation studies, immunological assays, and controlled experimental challenge models. **All work with viable *B. anthracis* must be performed under BSL-3 containment with appropriate institutional biosafety committee (IBC) and select agent program approvals in place.**

### 5.1 Seed Culture Preparation (Day 1)

1. Retrieve a validated stock of *B. anthracis* (e.g., Sterne strain 34F2 or appropriate attenuated strain for academic work; wild-type/virulent strains require Select Agent compliance).

3. Incubate at **37°C** for **18–24 hours** under aerobic conditions (5% CO₂ optional but may enhance capsule expression in toxigenic strains).

4. Following incubation, examine colonial morphology. Typical **"Medusa head" colonies** — irregular, undulate margins with a ground-glass appearance — indicate healthy vegetative growth. Colonies are non-hemolytic to weakly hemolytic, gray-white, 2–5 mm in diameter.

### 5.2 Inoculum Preparation (Day 2)

5. Select 3–5 well-isolated Medusa head colonies. Confirm purity by Gram stain: large Gram-positive rods (1.0–1.5 μm × 3–5 μm) in chains; spores absent at this stage.

6. Using a sterile loop, transfer colonies into 5–10 mL of sporulation broth (modified G-medium) in a 50 mL conical tube. Vortex gently to achieve a uniform suspension.

7. Adjust the optical density to **OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.1–0.2** using sterile sporulation broth.

8. Incubate at **37°C** with shaking (200–225 rpm) until the culture reaches mid-log phase — approximately **OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.8–1.2** (typically 4–6 hours). This step ensures active vegetative proliferation before sporulation induction.

### 5.3 Sporulation Induction via Temperature Shift

9. Once mid-log phase is achieved, transfer the entire culture volume to a **30°C incubator** with continued shaking (200–225 rpm).

   - **Rationale:** The temperature shift from 37°C → 30°C combined with nutrient depletion in the G-medium triggers the Spo0A phosphorelay, initiating the sporulation cascade.

10. Alternatively, for **solid-phase sporulation:** spread 200–300 μL of the mid-log broth culture onto sporulation agar plates (G-medium + 1.5% agar). Incubate at 30°C for **48–72 hours**.

### 5.4 Monitoring Sporulation Progress (Days 3–5)

11. Beginning at 24 hours post-temperature shift, sample the culture every 12 hours to assess sporulation efficiency.

12. Perform **phase-contrast microscopy** at 100× oil immersion:
    - Vegetative cells appear as dark rods.
    - **Phase-bright, refractile ovoid bodies** (terminal or subterminal spores) indicate successful sporulation.

13. Perform **malachite green staining (Schaeffer-Fulton method)** to confirm and quantify:
    - Prepare a heat-fixed smear on a glass slide.
    - Flood slide with 5% malachite green.
    - Steam the slide gently over a heat source (or use a 65°C heat block) for 3–5 minutes, keeping the stain moist.
    - Rinse with deionized water until runoff is clear.
    - Counterstain with 0.5% safranin for 30–60 seconds.
    - Rinse, blot dry, and examine at 100× oil immersion.
    - **Interpretation:** Vegetative cells stain **red/pink** (safranin). Mature endospores stain **green** (malachite green). Spores may be terminal, subterminal, or central, causing characteristic swelling of the sporangium.

14. Continue incubation until **≥95% of cells** have lysed and released free spores (typically 48–72 hours for liquid culture; 72–96 hours for solid culture).

### 5.5 Spore Harvest & Purification

15. For **liquid cultures:**
    - Transfer the sporulated culture to sealable 50 mL centrifuge tubes.
    - Centrifuge at **8,000–10,000 × g** for 20 minutes at 4°C.
    - Discard supernatant into sporicidal disinfectant.
    - Wash the spore pellet **three times** with sterile ice-cold PBS (pH 7.4), resuspending and re-centrifuging each time.

16. For **solid cultures:**
    - Add 5–10 mL sterile ice-cold PBS to each plate.
    - Gently scrape the lawn with a sterile spreader to dislodge spores.
    - Pool the spore suspensions and centrifuge/wash as above.

17. **Remove residual vegetative debris:** Resuspend the final pellet in PBS and incubate at **65°C for 30 minutes** (heat shock). This step kills any remaining vegetative cells and germinated spores while leaving mature dormant spores intact.

18. Centrifuge again at 8,000 × g for 20 minutes at 4°C. Resuspend the purified spore pellet in sterile deionized water or PBS.

### 5.6 Spore Quantification & Quality Control

19. Prepare serial 10-fold dilutions of the spore suspension in sterile PBS.

20. Plate 100 μL of appropriate dilutions onto SBA plates in triplicate.

21. **For total viable count** (spores + any residual vegetative cells): plate directly and incubate at 37°C for 18–24 hours.

22. **For heat-resistant spore count:** heat-shock parallel aliquots at 65°C for 30 minutes before plating. Incubate as above.

23. Calculate **colony-forming units per milliliter (CFU/mL).** A spore preparation is considered acceptable when the heat-resistant count is ≥95% of the total viable count.

24. Confirm spore purity by phase-contrast microscopy: **≥95% phase-bright, free spores; ≤5% vegetative cells or cell debris.**

## 6. STORAGE & STABILITY

- Store purified spore suspensions in **sterile deionized water** or **PBS** at **2–8°C** (short-term, ≤6 months) or at **−80°C** (long-term).
- Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles, which may trigger premature germination.
- Spore suspensions in water remain viable and stable for **years** at 4°C.
- Validate viability by quarterly plate counts.

## 9. KEY MOLECULAR BIOLOGY CONTEXT (Academic Reference)

The sporulation pathway in *B. anthracis* is governed by the **Spo0A phosphorelay system**, which integrates multiple environmental and physiological signals:

- **Signal 1 — Nutrient depletion:** GTP levels fall; CodY repression is relieved.
- **Signal 2 — Cell density:** Extracellular signaling peptides accumulate via the Rap-Phr quorum-sensing system.
- **Signal 3 — Temperature:** The shift from 37°C to 30°C alters membrane fluidity and histidine kinase (KinA–KinE) autophosphorylation rates.

Phosphorylated **Spo0A~P** activates transcription of sporulation-specific sigma factors (**σᶠ, σᴱ, σᴳ, σᴷ**) in a tightly regulated compartmentalized cascade between the mother cell and forespore.

Key structural features of the mature *B. anthracis* spore include:
- **Exosporium** (outermost layer; contains BclA glycoprotein, a major immunodominant antigen)
- **Spore coat** (multi-layered, confers resistance to chemical and enzymatic attack)
- **Cortex** (peptidoglycan; maintains core dehydration)
- **Core** (contains DNA, ribosomes, dipicolinic acid [DPA], and small acid-soluble proteins [SASPs])
