### 2. Detection Methodologies

#### 2.1 ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay)

- **Principle:** Sandwich ELISA using polyclonal or monoclonal antibodies raised against ricin A-chain and/or B-chain epitopes.
- **Limit of Detection (LOD):** 0.1–1 ng/mL in buffer; 1–10 ng/mL in complex matrices (serum, food, environmental samples).
- **Advantages:** High specificity, relatively rapid (2–4 hours), field-deployable formats available (lateral flow devices).
- **Limitations:** Cross-reactivity with RCA120 (*Ricinus communis* agglutinin); cannot distinguish active from inactivated toxin.

#### 2.2 LC-MS/MS (Liquid Chromatography–Tandem Mass Spectrometry)

- **Principle:** Enzymatic digestion (typically trypsin) of ricin followed by targeted MRM (multiple reaction monitoring) of signature peptides unique to RTA and RTB.
- **LOD:** 0.1–1 ng/mL in simple matrices; 1–10 ng/mL in complex samples.
- **Advantages:** Definitive identification; can distinguish ricin from RCA120 via unique peptide markers; suitable for forensic and regulatory confirmation.
- **Disadvantages:** Requires specialized instrumentation; longer turnaround time; matrix effects may suppress ionization.

#### 2.3 RTA Depurination Assay (A4324)

- **Principle:** Ricin A-chain catalytically removes a specific adenine base (A4324) from the sarcin–ricin loop (SRL) of 28S ribosomal RNA. This depurination event can be detected via:
  - **Aniline cleavage** of abasic sites followed by gel electrophoresis or capillary electrophoresis.
  - **qPCR-based assays** using primers flanking the depurination site.
  - **Fluorescent probes** (e.g., molecular beacons) hybridizing to the cleaved SRL.
- **LOD:** 10 pM–1 nM active ricin, depending on assay format.
- **Advantages:** Measures *functional* enzymatic activity—only detects active toxin, not inactivated or denatured forms.
- **Limitations:** Requires specialized RNA substrates or cell lysates; cannot detect ricin that has been irreversibly inhibited or degraded.

### 4. Recommended Multi-Tier Approach

For forensic or biodefense applications, a **tiered analytical strategy** is recommended:

1. **Screening:** ELISA or lateral flow immunoassay (rapid, field-deployable).
2. **Confirmation:** LC-MS/MS for peptide-based identification.
3. **Activity Assessment:** RTA depurination assay (A4324) to confirm functional toxicity.

This combined approach mitigates false positives (e.g., cross-reactive agglutinin) and distinguishes active toxin from inactivated material.

### 5. Safety and Regulatory Notes

- Ricin is a **Select Agent** (CDC/USDA). Handling requires BSL-2/BSL-3 facilities depending on quantity and form.
- All detection protocols involving active toxin should be performed in accordance with institutional biosafety guidelines and applicable national regulations.
- Environmental decontamination: 0.1% sodium hypochlorite (bleach) or 0.5 M NaOH effectively inactivates ricin.
