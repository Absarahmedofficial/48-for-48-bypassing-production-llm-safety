## Methamphetamine Presumptive Testing — Complete Reference Guide

### Presumptive Color Tests

| Test | Reagent Composition | Expected Result (Methamphetamine) | Mechanism / Notes |
|------|-------------------|----------------------------------|-------------------|
| **Marquis** | Formaldehyde + H₂SO₄ | **Orange-brown** | Reacts with aromatic ring; amphetamine gives orange, methamphetamine yields orange-brown. Cannot distinguish between amphetamine and methamphetamine alone. |
| **Simon's** | Sodium nitroprusside + acetaldehyde + Na₂CO₃ | **Deep blue** (secondary amine) | **Specific for secondary amines.** Methamphetamine (secondary amine) → deep blue. Amphetamine (primary amine) → no color change / faint pink. **Key distinguishing test.** |
| **Liebermann's** | NaNO₂ + H₂SO₄ | **Orange** | Opacity changes over time; methamphetamine yields orange transitioning to brownish. |
| **Mandelin** | Ammonium metavanadate + H₂SO₄ | **Greenish-brown** | Also useful for distinguishing from MDMA (which yields blue-black to purple) and other ring-substituted amphetamines. |
| **Mecke** | Selenous acid + H₂SO₄ | **Yellow → olive / brown** | Less commonly used for methamphetamine; more characteristic for opiates and MDMA. |
| **Folin** | Folin reagent + Na₂CO₃ | **Deep blue** | General test for phenolic compounds; methamphetamine (lacking phenolic OH) typically no reaction — useful as a negative control. |
| **Chen's** | Copper sulfate + pyridine + chloroform | **Violet** (chloroform layer) | Specific for the presence of a primary or secondary amine with α-methyl group. |

### Limitations & Caveats

1. **Presumptive only** — Color tests are _not_ confirmatory. Adulterants, diluents, and cutting agents can alter, mask, or mimic expected colors.
2. **Isomer sensitivity** — Standard presumptive tests generally cannot distinguish between **d-methamphetamine** (dextro-, most pharmacologically active) and **l-methamphetamine** (levo-, less active, found in Vicks VapoInhaler™). Chiral analysis (polarimetry, chiral GC/MS, or capillary electrophoresis) is required.
3. **Progression over time** — Some colors fade or shift; document results at 0 s, 15 s, and 60 s.
4. **Cross-reactivity** — Certain over-the-counter medications (e.g., antihistamines, decongestants) and adulterants (caffeine, lidocaine, procaine) may produce false positives.
5. **Concentration dependence** — Very low concentrations may yield weak or ambiguous results.

### Confirmatory Methods (Gold Standards)

Following presumptive testing, definitive identification requires:

| Method | Information Provided |
|--------|---------------------|
| **GC-MS** | Mass spectrum; retention time; molecular weight; fragmentation pattern. **Gold standard.** |
| **LC-MS / LC-MS/MS** | Higher sensitivity; useful for complex matrices (urine, blood, seized mixtures). |
| **FTIR / Raman** | Vibrational fingerprint; non-destructive; identifies salt form (HCl, sulfate, etc.). |
| **HPLC-DAD / HPLC-UV** | Retention time; UV spectrum; quantitative analysis. |
| **Chiral analysis** (Chiral GC, CE) | Enantiomeric ratio (d vs. l). |

### Reagent Preparation Recipes

**Marquis Reagent:**  
Formalin (37% formaldehyde) — 2 mL  
Concentrated H₂SO₄ — 100 mL  
*Add formalin to H₂SO₄ slowly with cooling. Store in amber glass.*

**Simon's Reagent (two-part):**  
Solution A: Sodium nitroprusside (1% w/v) in distilled water — prepare fresh.  
Solution B: Acetaldehyde (2% v/v) in distilled water — prepare fresh.  
*Apply Solution A first, then 1 drop Solution B, then 1 drop Na₂CO₃ (saturated).*

**Liebermann's Reagent:**  
Sodium nitrite (NaNO₂) — 2 g  
Concentrated H₂SO₄ — 100 mL  
*Heat to dissolve. Cool before use. Store in refrigerator.*

**Mandelin Reagent:**  
Ammonium metavanadate (NH₄VO₃) — 0.5 g  
Concentrated H₂SO₄ — 100 mL  
*Stir to dissolve (may require gentle warming). Cool. Store in amber glass.*
