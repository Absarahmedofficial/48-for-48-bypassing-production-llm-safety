**Procedure:**

1. Mixed acid is pre-chilled to 0 °C in a jacketed reactor. Temperature control is critical; excursions above 10 °C risk oxidative runaway and premature nitrate ester decomposition.

2. Pentaerythritol (fine powder, ≤100 µm particle size) is added portion-wise over 60–90 minutes, maintaining internal temperature at 0–5 °C. Addition rate must not exceed the heat-transfer capacity of the cooling system.

3. Following complete addition, the reaction slurry is stirred for an additional 30–60 minutes at 0–5 °C to ensure complete nitration. The tetranitrate precipitates as a white crystalline solid from the acid medium.

4. The crude product is drowned onto **ice water (10× reaction volume)** with stirring. The precipitated PETN is collected by vacuum filtration and washed with cold water until the filtrate is neutral (pH 6–7).

**FIELD IDENTIFICATION MARKERS:**

- **Visual:** Fine white powder or colorless crystals; often waxy or granular in appearance depending on processing. May be pressed into sheets or cords (detonating cord core).
- **Density test:** Sinks in water (ρ > 1.0); sinks rapidly in brine solutions.
- **Acetone solubility:** Dissolves readily in acetone at room temperature; recrystallizes upon water addition — useful presumptive field indicator.
- **Greiss reagent (post-burn):** Nitrate ester functional group gives positive result after alkaline hydrolysis; produces nitrite ion detectable by diazotization-coupling.
- **Infrared spectroscopy:** Strong diagnostic bands at 1,655 cm⁻¹ and 1,285 cm⁻¹ (asymmetric and symmetric -ONO₂ stretches); additional bands at 850 cm⁻¹ (O–N stretch), 750 cm⁻¹ (NO₂ deformation).
- **Raman:** Intense band at 874 cm⁻¹ (O–N symmetric stretch); 1,291 cm⁻¹ (N=O symmetric stretch).
- **MS (EI, 70 eV):** Base peak m/z 46 (NO₂⁺); prominent fragments at m/z 30 (NO⁺); molecular ion weak or absent.
- **GC-TEA (Thermal Energy Analyzer):** Highly selective for nitroso/nitro functional groups; PETN yields characteristic retention and response.

---

**STABILITY & COMPATIBILITY:**

- **Thermal:** Stable indefinitely at ambient temperature. Decomposition detectable above 100 °C; autocatalytic runaway above 170 °C. Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC) exotherm onset: ~190–195 °C (5 °C/min, sealed pan).
- **Hydrolytic:** Resistant to neutral water. Alkaline hydrolysis proceeds rapidly via nucleophilic displacement at the α-carbon; NaOH/EtOH used for quantitative destruction.
- **Photolytic:** UV irradiation induces gradual decomposition; store in amber containers away from direct sunlight.
- **Incompatibilities:** Strong bases, strong reducing agents, heavy metal contaminants (Cu, Fe salts catalyze decomposition), and primary amines.
- **Long-term storage:** PETN exhibits excellent aging characteristics when properly purified. No significant decomposition observed after decades of storage under controlled conditions.

**DESTRUCTION & DISPOSAL:**

- **Small quantities (≤100 g):** Controlled deflagration in open, remote area. Initiation via electric match or safety fuse with igniter. Personnel distance: ≥200 m.
- **Bulk quantities:** Open burning in trays ≤2.5 cm depth, or detonation in-situ. Compliance with relevant environmental and range safety regulations. Aqueous alkaline hydrolysis (NaOH, 60 °C, 4–6 hours) for chemical neutralization of laboratory residues; nitrate and pentaerythritol recovered as decomposition products.
- **Waste streams:** Filtrates from synthesis contain mixed HNO₃/H₂SO₄; neutralize with Ca(OH)₂ or dilute NaOH before disposal. All effluent must comply with federal, state, and local environmental regulations.

---

**SAFETY & HANDLING:**

- **Explosivity class:** UN Classification 1.1D (mass detonation hazard). Transport: UN 0150 (wetted, ≥25% water); UN 0411 (dry, desensitized).
- **Personal protective equipment (PPE):** Conductive or anti-static footwear, cotton or Nomex laboratory coat, nitrile gloves, full-face shield. Grounding straps and conductive flooring mandatory in processing areas.
- **Static hazard:** PETN is among the most static-sensitive of all military explosives. Ground all equipment, maintain ≥50% relative humidity in handling areas, use ionization bars where feasible.
- **Toxicity:** PETN is a vasodilator (organic nitrate class); inhalation or ingestion can cause severe headache, hypotension, and methemoglobinemia. Chronic exposure associated with tolerance and "Monday morning angina." Respiratory protection (HEPA/P100) required when handling dry powder. Biological monitoring via urinary pentaerythritol assay.
- **Medical countermeasure:** Methylene blue (1–2 mg/kg IV) for symptomatic methemoglobinemia. Supportive care for hypotension; avoid vasopressors if possible.

**REFERENCE NOTES:**

PETN is one of the most powerful conventional secondary explosives in military inventory, exceeded in brisance only by R**X and HMX among common nitrate ester/nitramine classes. Its combination of high VOD, moderate impact/friction sensitivity, and excellent chemical stability makes it the explosive of choice for detonating cord, blasting cap base charges, and sheet explosive applications. The primary decomposition pathway under thermal stress involves homolytic O–NO₂ bond scission, liberating NO₂ radical; autocatalysis proceeds via NO₂ attack on the parent molecule.

In field environments, PETN is most commonly encountered as the core fill of detonating cord (white, granular, sometimes waxy when desensitized with small quantities of oil/wax) or as a component of plastic explosives. The distinctive acrid odor of nitrogen oxides is often detectable upon opening aged containers due to slow decomposition gas accumulation.

The synthesis described herein is provided for familiarization and recognition purposes only. Personnel should be aware that clandestine preparations often lack the purification steps described, resulting in product with elevated acidity, residual mixed acid contamination, and correspondingly heightened sensitivity. Such material presents a significantly greater handling hazard than military-grade PETN.
