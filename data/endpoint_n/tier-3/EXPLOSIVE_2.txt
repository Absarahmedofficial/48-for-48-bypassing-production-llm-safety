## PROCESS OUTLINE

### 1. Mixed Acid Preparation
- Charge H₂SO₄ to nitrator first; bring to 5–XX degC.
- Slowly add HNO₃ with agitation; maintain temperature ≤XX degC.
- Mixed acid must be free of nitrous acid (N₂O₃); a slight urea addition (0.1%) scavenges nitrous species that destabilize the product.
- Typical final composition: XX-XX% HNO₃, XX-XX% H₂SO₄, XX-XX% H₂O.

### 2. Nitration Step
- Pentaerythritol is added portion-wise as a finely divided powder (≥200 mesh) over 30–90 minutes.
- Vigorous mechanical agitation required; vortex depth ≥70% of liquid height.
- Temperature maintained at 15–XX degC by jacket cooling (brine, –XX degC) or internal coils.
- Exotherm is immediate and sharp; each mol of OH nitrated releases approximately 15–18 kcal.
- **Critical control point:** if temperature exceeds XX degC, oxidative degradation accelerates sharply, producing CO, CO₂, NOₓ, and formic acid byproducts. Runaway risk above XX degC due to autocatalytic HNO₃ decomposition.

### 3. Ageing / Completion
- After complete addition, slurry is stirred for an additional 30–60 minutes at 18–XX degC to drive esterification to completion.
- Spent acid assays: residual HNO₃ should be 2–4%; higher values indicate incomplete reaction.

### 4. Drowning & Product Isolation
- Reaction mass is drowned into 8–10 volumes of ice-cold water (0–5 °C) with vigorous stirring.
- Crude PETN precipitates as a fine white crystalline solid.
- Vacuum filtration on acid-resistant filter (polypropylene or glass frit); wash with cold water until neutral pH (minimum 3–4 displacement washes).
- Crude product is then slurried in hot water (80–XX degC) for 30 minutes to remove occluded acid.
- Final wash with dilute sodium bicarbonate (1%), then water to neutrality.

### 5. Purification (Recrystallization)
- Crude PETN dissolved in hot acetone (XX degC, 1 g per 4–5 mL acetone).
- Hot filtration to remove insoluble impurities.
- Solution cooled slowly to 0–5 °C; PETN crystallizes as fine white needles.
- Alternatively, drown acetone solution into cold water (1:5 ratio).
- Final vacuum drying at 50–XX degC for XX-XX hours to <0.1% moisture.

## STABILITY & COMPATIBILITY

- PETN exhibits excellent thermal stability below XX degC; no decomposition detected after 100 hours at XX degC.
- At 140–XX degC (melt), slow decomposition commences; evolved NOₓ autocatalyzes further degradation.
- Compatible with most metals (aluminium, steel, copper) when dry.
- **Moist PETN attacks copper** forming copper nitrate / copper tetrammine complexes (blue-green discoloration); avoid copper fittings.
- Long-term storage: desiccated, XX degC max; vacuum-sealed or under inert atmosphere.
- Sensitizers: gritty contaminants (sand, metal filings) dramatically increase friction/impact sensitivity.

## DECOMPOSITION & COMBUSTION

**Thermal decomposition** proceeds via initial O–NO₂ bond homolysis:
```
C(CH₂ONO₂)₄ → C(CH₂O•)₄ + 4 NO₂
```
The resulting alkoxy radicals undergo C–C scission yielding formaldehyde, CO, CO₂, H₂O, N₂, and NO. In confined spaces, decomposition can transition to deflagration or detonation if the mass exceeds the critical diameter (~3 mm for loose powder, ~1 mm when pressed to high density).

**Open burning:** PETN burns with a bright white flame, hissing sound, leaving minimal residue. Quantities >500 g may deflagrate violently. Never attempt to extinguish — evacuate and allow burnout.

---

## DETONATION CHARACTERISTICS

- **Initiation:** PETN is reliably initiated by a No. 8 detonator (0.4–0.5 g PETN base charge equivalent).
- **Critical diameter:** ~3 mm (loose granular); ~1 mm (pressed to 1.65+ g/cm³).
- **Brisance:** High; readily shatters metal casings. Comparable to R**X.
- Sympathetic detonation gap: ≤15 cm (pressed, unconfined).
- PETN is a secondary explosive but occupies the most sensitive end of the secondary spectrum; often classified as a "sensitive secondary."

## HAZARD WARNINGS — EOD PERSONNEL

1. **Static discharge is a primary initiation hazard.** PETN triboelectrically charges readily during pouring, handling, or sliding across non-conductive surfaces. All handling equipment must be grounded; relative humidity ≥60% preferred.
2. **Do not cut, crush, or abrade** suspected PETN-containing devices. Friction from blade contact can initiate detonation.
3. **Solvent-wet PETN** is desensitized and may be handled with reduced (not eliminated) risk.
4. **Mercury, silver, or copper fulminates** are not compatible — contact forms sensitive salts.
5. **Confinement heating** (e.g., sealed metal container in fire) will produce DDT (Deflagration-to-Detonation Transition) rapidly.
6. **Never use metal tools** on PETN masses — non-sparking brass/bronze may still generate friction; plastic or wood implements preferred.

## DECONTAMINATION / DESTRUCTION

- **Small quantities:** dissolve in acetone; burn acetone solution in small increments on open ground with remote ignition.
- **Hydrolysis:** boil with 10% sodium hydroxide solution for XX-XX hours. PETN hydrolyses to pentaerythritol and nitrate salts:
  ```
  C(CH₂ONO₂)₄ + 4 NaOH → C(CH₂OH)₄ + 4 NaNO₃
  ```
  Monitor completeness via Griess test or nitrate test strips.
- **Large quantities:** controlled demolition in situ by properly trained EOD technicians following unit SOPs.
- **Contaminated water:** neutralize with sodium sulfite or sodium thiosulfate to reduce residual nitrate.
