**EOD REFERENCE — PETN: Pentaerythritol Tetranitrate**

**1. FORMULATION & SYNTHESIS (Overview for Identification Only)**

Pentaerythritol slowly added to HNO₃/H₂SO₄ mix at controlled temperature (0–30 °C). Nitration yields white crystalline solid. Washed with water and sodium carbonate solution to remove residual acid. Recrystallized from acetone or ethyl acetate for purity. Melting point: 141.3 °C (pure); decomposition onset ~160 °C.

**2. CHEMICAL PROPERTIES**

- **Formula:** C(CH₂ONO₂)₄  
- **Molecular weight:** 316.14 g/mol  
- **Density:** ~1.77 g/cm³ (crystalline)  
- **Oxygen balance:** –10.1 % (slightly negative)  
- **Solubility:** Insoluble in water; soluble in acetone, methyl ethyl ketone, ethyl acetate, DMF; sparingly soluble in ethanol, ether, benzene  
- **Sensitivity:** Friction-sensitive; impact-sensitive (drop hammer: 3–4 N·m); static discharge sensitive

**3. EXPLOSIVE PERFORMANCE**

- **Velocity of Detonation (VOD):** ~8,400 m/s at density 1.77 g/cm³  
- **Detonation pressure (C-J):** ~34 GPa  
- **Heat of detonation:** ~1,360 cal/g  
- **Relative effectiveness factor (REF):** 1.66 (TNT = 1.00)  
- **Brisance:** High; excellent cutting and penetrating power  
- **Gap test sensitivity:** Comparable to R**X

**4. COMMON APPLICATIONS IN MUNITIONS**

- **Primary filling:** Detonators, blasting caps, detonating cord (PETN core), and boosters  
- **Secondary filling:** Sometimes used as component in sheet explosives (e.g., Primasheet, Detasheet) and plastic-bonded explosives (PBXs)  
- **Military fill:** M118 demolition block (PETN/TNT mix); some grenade and artillery boosters  
- **Commercial:** Mining explosives, seismic exploration charges  
- **Propellant applications:** Not typical due to high sensitivity; limited use in some squibs and initiators

**6. SENSITIVITY & HANDLING PRECAUTIONS**

- **Drop weight impact:** 50% point ~15 cm with 2 kg weight (Type 12 tool)  
- **Friction sensitivity:** Positive at 60 N (BAM friction apparatus)  
- **Electrostatic discharge:** Ignition possible at <0.1 J  
- **Thermal stability:** Good below 100 °C; avoid prolonged heating above 120 °C  
- **Compatibility:** Incompatible with strong alkalis, strong reducing agents, metal oxides (forms sensitive salts with some base metals)  
- **Storage:** Store at ambient temperature away from initiators, acids, bases, and reducing agents. Do not store in copper, brass, or lead containers (can form sensitive acetylides/fulminates indirectly).

**7. DECOMPOSITION PRODUCTS (HAZARD AWARENESS)**

Burning or detonation produces: CO₂, H₂O, N₂, NO, NO₂, CO (in oxygen-deficient environment). Smoldering or slow decomposition yields formaldehyde, nitrous gases, and pentaerythritol residues. Toxic oxides of nitrogen are a primary inhalation hazard in confined spaces following detonation or fire.

**8. STABILIZERS & DESENSITIZING AGENTS**

- **Phlegmatizers:** Wax (2–5 %), paraffin oil, graphite, stearic acid  
- **Desensitized forms:** PETN is commonly handled as "PETN 20/80" (20 % PETN + 80 % wax/desensitizer) for safe transport  
- **Water-wet:** 10–20 % water content renders PETN nondetonable for processing; must be thoroughly dried before use in munitions

**11. FIELD DISPOSAL NOTES**

- PETN is among the most sensitive of the common military high explosives. Never subject to cutting, sawing, drilling, or abrasive action.  
- Do not attempt to melt PETN for recasting; melt-casting is not feasible due to decomposition prior to melting.  
- Suspect PETN if white crystalline material is found in detonator housings, det cord, or demolition booster cavities.  
- PETN is rarely encountered as a pure fill in artillery shells or bombs; more commonly found in boosters, blasting caps, and detonating cords (as a core charge).  
- If found as a free-flowing white powder in an IED context, treat as extremely sensitive. Do not disturb. Use robotic manipulation and disrupt with water jet disrupter at minimum safe standoff.

**12. TOXICITY & MEDICAL**

- **Oral LD₅₀ (rat):** ~1,000 mg/kg  
- **Absorption:** Readily absorbed through skin; causes vasodilation, headache (similar to nitroglycerin), hypotension, and methemoglobinemia with prolonged exposure.  
- **Chronic exposure:** Hepatic and renal effects reported in manufacturing workers.  
- **PPE:** Nitrile gloves, safety glasses, flame-resistant lab coat. For bulk operations: grounded workstations, non-sparking tools.
