**R**cin (cont.)**

**EXTRACTION & PURIFICATION**

Castor beans crushed, defatted. Aqueous extraction yields crude r**cin in supernatant. Salting out (e.g., (NH₄)₂SO₄ fractionation, XX-XX% saturation) precipitates protein fraction containing r**cin. Further purification: ion-exchange chromatography (CM-cellulose, DEAE), gel filtration (Sephadex G-75/G-100), or affinity chromatography (lactose-agarose — r**cin is a galactose/N-acetylgalactosamine-binding lectin). R**cin exists as two isoforms: r**cin D (predominant in *R**cinus communis* var. *zanzibariensis*) and r**cin E. Purity assessed by SDS-PAGE (≈65 kDa heterodimer: ~32 kDa A-chain, ~34 kDa B-chain, disulfide-linked).

**TOXICODYNAMICS**

R**cin toxin (RCA60; type 2 ribosome-inactivating protein, RIP). Mechanism: B-chain (lectin) binds cell-surface glycoproteins/glycolipids bearing terminal β-1,4-linked galactose → endocytosis → retrograde transport via Golgi → endoplasmic reticulum. A-chain (N-glycosidase) depurinates a specific adenine residue (A4324 in rat 28S rRNA, homologous position in human 28S rRNA) within the sarcin-r**cin loop (SRL) of the 60S ribosomal subunit. This depurination irreversibly inhibits elongation factor-2 (EF-2) binding, arresting protein synthesis. One A-chain molecule can inactivate ~1,500 ribosomes/minute — catalytic, highly potent.

**TOXICOKINETICS**

- **Inhalation:** Most lethal route. Aerosolized r**cin (particle size 1–3 µm) deposits in alveoli and terminal bronchioles. Rapid uptake by alveolar macrophages and epithelial cells. LD₅₀ (non-human primates): 5–10 µg/kg. Onset: 8–24 h post-exposure.
- **Ingestion:** Mucosal damage; absorption variable. LD₅₀ (mouse oral): X-X mg/kg — substantially less potent due to digestive proteolysis and poor systemic absorption. Gastric decontamination critical if early.
- **Injection:** LD₅₀ (mouse i.p./i.v.): 1–5 µg/kg. Direct vascular access yields rapid distribution to liver, spleen, RES.
- **Intramuscular / Subcutaneous:** Local necrosis, regional lymph node involvement; slower systemic absorption.

**CLINICAL SYNDROMES BY ROUTE**

**Inhalation (most critical for BW/CBRN concern):**
- Prodrome (4–8 h): Malaise, fever, non-productive cough, myalgias.
- Phase 2 (12–24 h): Progressive dyspnea, tachypnea, bilateral interstitial infiltrates on CXR ("chemical pneumonitis"), hypoxia, hemoptysis.
- Phase 3 (24–72 h): Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), diffuse alveolar damage, pulmonary edema, hypotension, multi-organ failure → death.

**Ingestion:**
- Immediate: Oropharyngeal irritation, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, watery/bloody diarrhea.
- 24–72 h: Hepatic necrosis (elevated AST/ALT/GGT), renal tubular necrosis, GI hemorrhage, hypovolemic shock, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC).

**Injection:**
- Local necrosis, lymphadenopathy.
- Systemic: Malaise, fever, hepatic/renal failure, DIC, hypotensive shock.

**Ocular:**
- Conjunctivitis, chemosis, periorbital edema, corneal ulceration, anterior uveitis.

**DETECTION / DIAGNOSIS**

- **Time-sensitive:** R**cin is immunogenic but rapidly cleared (t½ α ~15 min; β ~4 h in plasma). Early sampling essential.
- **ELISA:** Detection limit ~1 ng/mL in buffer; ~0.1–1 ng/mL in serum (sandwich ELISA, anti-r**cin monoclonal/polyclonal antibodies). Cross-reactivity with RCA₁₂⁰ (R**cinus communis agglutinin, a non-toxic tetrameric lectin) is possible — confirmatory testing needed.
- **Mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS):** Gold standard for confirmation. Tryptic digestion → detection of unique peptide signatures (e.g., from A- and B-chains). Estimated detection limit: sub-nanogram amounts.
- **PCR (forensic):** Detection of *R**cinus* DNA as proxy for r**cin presence (not a direct toxin assay).
- **Biological activity assays:**
  - *Cell-free translation inhibition assay* (rabbit reticulocyte lysate): Functional assay, highly sensitive (~1 pg/mL).
  - *Lactose-agarose pull-down + LC-MS*: Confirms lectin activity + identity.
- **Immunohistochemistry (tissue):** Detection of r**cin in fixed tissues (lung, liver, spleen, lymphoid tissue).

**PROPHYLAXIS / THERAPEUTICS**

**Pre-exposure:**
- No licensed human vaccine. Candidate vaccines: RiVax® (recombinant A-chain, mutated Y80A, alum-adjuvanted) and RVEc™ (truncated A + B chains). In animal models, protective IgG titers achieved.
- Equine anti-r**cin F(ab')₂ antiserum (ovine or equine) under investigation.

**Post-exposure:**
- **Supportive care only** (no approved antitoxin in U.S. for human use):
  - Airway management, mechanical ventilation, aggressive fluid resuscitation.
  - Activated charcoal (if ingestion within 1 h) + whole-bowel irrigation if massive ingestion.
  - Vasopressors, renal replacement therapy as needed.
- **Experimental:**
  - Polyclonal/monoclonal antitoxin (e.g., huRtx, chimeric mAb). Greatest efficacy if administered within 4–6 h post-exposure.
  - Small-molecule inhibitors of retrograde transport (e.g., Retro-2, Retro-2.1) — preclinical.
  - Ribosome protection / depurination inhibitors — not yet in clinical use.

**DECONTAMINATION**

- **Skin:** Copious water + mild soap. 0.5% hypochlorite (dilute bleach) solution for surfaces; **do not use bleach on skin** — may enhance absorption.
- **Mucous membranes/eyes:** Saline irrigation for 15–20 min.
- **Environmental:** R**cin is a protein; denatured by heat (>XX degC, 30 min), autoclaving (XX degC, 20 min), or strong alkali (e.g., 1 M NaOH, 1 h). Hypochlorite (≥0.2% free chlorine) or 1% sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) + heat also effective. R**cin persists on dry surfaces for days–weeks; stability enhanced in dry, cool, dark conditions.
