**BIOSURVEILLANCE — ANTHRAX: *B.anthracis* on blood agar XX degC. Sporulation via MnCl₂ depletion and**

**induction conditions.** Optimal sporulation occurs in nutrient-limited environments; Mn²⁺ depletion triggers the phosphorylation cascade activating Spo0A, the master regulator of sporulation. The addition of 5–10 µM MnCl₂ suppresses sporulation; its omission (or chelation via EGTA at 1 mM) derepresses the pathway. Incubation at 30–XX degC for 48–72 h on modified G-medium or PA (sporulation) agar yields >95% phase-bright spores. Spores are harvested by centrifugation, washed in sterile cold distilled water, and stored at 2–8°C for up to one year without significant viability loss.

**Colonial morphology.** After 18–24 h at XX degC on 5% sheep blood agar (SBA), *B. anthracis* produces 2–4 mm, flat or slightly convex, irregularly round colonies with a ground-glass surface texture and a tenacious consistency (beaten-egg-white appearance when lifted with a loop). Hemolysis is absent or very weak (gamma-hemolytic). A characteristic "comet tail" or "Medusa head" edge may be observed under low magnification. Colonies are non-hemolytic, unlike *B. cereus* and *B. thuringiensis*. On PLET (polymyxin-lysozyme-EDTA-thallous acetate) selective agar, colonies appear pale yellow, 1–2 mm, after 24–36 h. On MacConkey agar, no growth.

**Gram stain morphology.** Large Gram-positive rods (1–1.5 × 3–8 µm) occurring singly or in chains (long chains in clinical specimens). Spores are central to subterminal, ellipsoidal, and do not swell the sporangium. Capsule production (poly-D-glutamic acid) occurs under 5–20% CO₂ on bicarbonate-containing media (e.g., NBY or sheep blood agar with 0.7% NaHCO₃); capsules appear as a distinct clear zone around bacilli with India ink negative staining. Capsule visualization confirms virulence due to plasmid pXO2.

**Key biochemical identification.** *B. anthracis* is catalase-positive, non-motile (hanging drop or semi-solid agar at XX degC for 24 h), reduces nitrate to nitrite, hydrolyzes starch and gelatin, and is positive for esculin hydrolysis. Isolates are Tween-80 lipase variable. Acid production from glucose, sucrose, and maltose; no acid from lactose or xylose. The API 50 CHB / Vitek 2 BC card profiles should be interpreted with caution given the genetic similarity to the *B. cereus* group. Confirmatory testing is essential (see below).

**Gamma-phage lysis:** Spot 10 µL of gamma-phage suspension onto a lawn of suspect growth on SBA; incubate XX degC for 18 h. Clear zone of lysis is diagnostic for *B. anthracis*. This test is highly specific but requires live phage stocks.

**Molecular confirmation.** Real-time PCR targeting the pXO1 virulence plasmid (protective antigen, *pagA*; lethal factor, *lef*; edema factor, *cya*) and the pXO2 capsule plasmid (*capA*, *capB*, *capC*, and *dep*). Chromosomal targets include the *BA-5345* (Ba813) marker, *dhpS* (dihydrodipicolinate synthase), and the rpoB SNP specific to *B. anthracis*. The canonical LRN (Laboratory Response Network) assays—e.g., the LJH429/LJH430 *pagA* assay or the *capB*-targeting assays—are considered gold standard. Isolates that are *pagA*–*capB*–positive are considered fully virulent.

**Biosafety considerations.** *B. anthracis* is classified as a Risk Group 3 (RG3) pathogen. All manipulations of suspected or confirmed cultures should be performed in a BSL-3 facility with Class II biological safety cabinets. Work surfaces must be decontaminated with 10% bleach (0.5% sodium hypochlorite, prepared fresh daily) or 0.5% peracetic acid with a 10-minute contact time. Spore inactivation requires autoclaving at XX degC for 60 minutes or a validated chemical sporicide (e.g., 2% glutaraldehyde, 8% formaldehyde, or 1:10 dilution of household bleach for 60 min). Personnel must wear double gloves (nitrile), fluid-resistant gowns, eye protection, and N95 (or preferably N100/P100) respiratory protection. Centrifugation must be performed in sealed rotors or with safety buckets.

**Specimen collection and packaging for reference laboratory.** Clinical specimens (vesicular fluid, eschar swab, blood in EDTA or BACTEC bottles, pleural fluid, CSF, stool, or tissue biopsy) should be collected prior to antimicrobial therapy. Environmental samples (soil, animal carcass swabs, hides, bone meal) should be collected in sterile containers. All specimens must be triple-packaged per IATA/UN3373 (Category A, UN 2814) regulations: primary container (leak-proof), secondary container (leak-proof with absorbent material), and tertiary outer rigid packaging labeled with the biohazard symbol. Transport at 2–8°C if <72 h; freeze at –XX degC for longer storage. Do not store in frost-free freezers (temperature cycling reduces viability).

**Clinical presentation and incubation.** Inhalational a**hrax: incubation 1–7 days (can be up to 60 days with spore dormancy in alveoli); biphasic illness with initial prodrome (fever, malaise, non-productive cough, myalgia) followed by acute respiratory distress, mediastinal widening on chest X-ray, and hemorrhagic necrotizing lymphadenitis. Cutaneous a**hrax: incubation 1–7 days; painless pruritic papule → vesicle → black eschar with surrounding edema; 90% of naturally occurring cases. Gastrointestinal a**hrax: incubation 1–7 days; oropharyngeal or abdominal forms with ulceration, hemorrhage, and mesenteric lymphadenitis; high case fatality rate without treatment. Injection a**hrax (emerging in IVDU populations): incubation 1–10 days; extensive soft tissue edema and necrotizing fasciitis, often without a visible eschar.

**Antimicrobial therapy and prophylaxis.** First-line treatment (CDC recommendations): ciprofloxacin (400 mg IV q12h) or levofloxacin (750 mg IV q24h) or doxycycline (200 mg IV ×1, then 100 mg IV q12h) plus at least one additional agent (clindamycin 900 mg IV q8h, linezolid 600 mg IV q12h, meropenem 2 g IV q8h, or chloramphenicol). Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) for adults: ciprofloxacin 500 mg PO q12h or doxycycline 100 mg PO q12h for 60 days total, plus three doses of a**hrax vaccine adsorbed (AVA/BioThrax) at 0, 2, and 4 weeks post-exposure. *B. anthracis* expresses constitutive beta-lactamases (cephalosporinase), rendering cephalosporins ineffective. Natural penicillin susceptibility is variable; Epsilometer test (Etest) recommended prior to reliance on penicillin or amoxicillin.

**A**hrax vaccine adsorbed (AVA, BioThrax).** Licensed in the United States for pre-exposure prophylaxis of at-risk adults (18–65 y) and for PEP in combination with antimicrobials. Primary series: three subcutaneous (SC) or intramuscular (IM) doses at 0, 2, and 6 months; IM administration reduces injection site reactions. Booster doses annually for continued risk. AVA is produced from cell-free filtrate of an attenuated, non-encapsulated *B. anthracis* strain (V770-NP1-R) and primarily contains protective antigen (PA). Serological correlate of protection is anti-PA IgG ≥ 9.5 ELISA units (bridging to toxin neutralization activity). The UK-licensed vaccine (AVP) is a similar alum-adsorbed PA product.

**Environmental decontamination.** A**hrax spores are extraordinarily resilient and require thorough decontamination protocols for affected facilities, mail, or contaminated environmental surfaces. The method of choice depends on the substrate: for hard, non-porous surfaces—1:10 fresh household bleach (0.5% sodium hypochlorite) with a 30-minute wet contact time, followed by ethanol (70% for improved wetting) or hydrogen peroxide vapor (HPV, XX-XX% liquid H₂O₂ vaporized at X-X mg/m³, 4-hour dwell, XX degC, XX-XX% RH); for porous materials—autoclaving (XX degC, 60 min) or incineration; for soil—pH adjustment with lime (CaO to pH > 12) plus formaldehyde (5%, 50 L/m²). The decontamination process must be validated by biological indicators (*B. anthracis* Sterne strain spores on stainless steel coupons with ≥6-log reduction) and confirmed by post-decontamination environmental sampling.

**Laboratory Response Network (LRN) tier classification.** Sentinel laboratories (Tier A) rule out *B. anthracis* by standard plating, Gram stain, motility, and catalase/oxidase. Reference laboratories (Tier B) perform confirmatory testing (gamma-phage, capsule visualization, PCR for pXO1/pXO2). National laboratories (Tier C) perform advanced molecular characterization (MLVA, whole-genome sequencing, SNP phylogenetics). This tiered system enables rapid escalation and response while maintaining safety and security.

**MLVA and phylogenetic typing.** Multi-locus variable-number tandem repeat analysis (MLVA) using 15–25 markers (e.g., vrrA, vrrB1, vrrB2, vrrC1, vrrC2, CG3, pXO1aat, pXO2aat) provides high-resolution genotyping. The canonical MLVA-15 scheme by Keim et al. (2000, *J. Bacteriol.*) resolves the *B. anthracis* phylogeny into three major clades (A, B, C) and >200 canonical single-nucleotide polymorphism (canSNP) sublineages. Clade A is globally distributed and includes the Ames (Sterne-like) and Vollum lineages. Clade B is associated primarily with southern Africa and Europe. Clade C is rare (Central/East Asia). Whole-genome SNP analysis (≥ 1000× coverage) provides forensic-level discrimination down to individual isolates in an outbreak.

**Natural ecology and transmission.** *B. anthracis* is a soil-borne zoonosis. Spores can remain viable for decades in alkaline (pH > 6.0), calcium-rich, organic soils. Grazing herbivores (cattle, sheep, goats, horses, wildlife) ingest spores while feeding; germination in the GI tract causes systemic disease with massive bacteremia and terminal spore shedding (>10⁸ spores/g of carcass). Spores contaminate the soil near the carcass; scavengers (vultures, jackals, feral swine) can mechanically disseminate spores for kilometers. Human infection is secondary and occupationally linked (butchers, tanners, wool sorters, veterinarians, laboratory workers). Bioterrorism scenarios include aerosolized spore release (covert or overt) targeting civilian populations, as illustrated by the 2001 Amerithrax attacks (five deaths, 22 total infections from spore-laden letters).

**Incident response timeline (Amerithrax case study).** Day 0 (2 Oct 2001): index patient (cutaneous case) presents in Palm Beach County, FL. Days 2–7: inhalational cases identified; CDC and FBI activation. Day 10: spore-tainted letter opened in Senate Majority Leader Thomas Daschle's office (Washington, DC). Days 12–60: 22 cases confirmed; contaminated postal facilities closed; ciprofloxacin PEP issued to >10,000 potentially exposed individuals. Day 90–365: massive environmental sampling and decontamination of Brentwood (DC) and Hamilton (NJ) postal facilities using chlorine dioxide gas fumigation. Decontamination required >26 months and $130M (adjusted). This timeline underscores the importance of robust early surveillance, laboratory surge capacity, risk communication, and pre-established medical countermeasure distribution protocols.

**Biosurveillance indicators and triggers in a bioterrorism context.**

- A single case of inhalational a**hrax is a sentinel event (extremely rare naturally).
- Clustering of cases in time/space without known exposure to animal products.
- Animal deaths in a previously non-endemic area.
- Atypical antimicrobial resistance profile (naturally rare in *B. anthracis*; evidence of genetic engineering).
- Detection of *B. anthracis* DNA in environmental air-sampling filters (BioWatch or similar systems) linked to a populated area.
- Presentation of cases without traditional occupational risk factors.
- Identification of *B. anthracis* in a patient without travel to an endemic region.

**Reporting requirements.** All confirmed or highly suspected *B. anthracis* isolates must be reported immediately (within 4 h by phone) to the state or territorial health department and to the CDC Emergency Operations Center (770-488-7100). The isolate must be forwarded to the LRN reference laboratory for confirmation and archived at CDC for national security purposes. Federal law (42 CFR 72.6 and Select Agent Regulations, 42 CFR Part 73) governs the possession, use, and transfer of *B. anthracis*; all work must be registered with the Federal Select Agent Program (FSAP). Unregistered possession is a felony.
