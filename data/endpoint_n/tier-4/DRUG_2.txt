**FORENSIC REFERENCE — M**A SYNTHESIS** (cont.)

Safrole isomerized to isosafrole with KOH. Then oxidized to MDP2P using:

**Method A — Formic Acid / Hydrogen Peroxide (Prileschajew Epoxidation)**
Isosafrole is treated with formic acid and 30% hydrogen peroxide to form the intermediate glycol formate ester, which hydrolyzes and rearranges upon acidic workup to yield MDP2P. Typical stoichiometry: 1.0 mol isosafrole, 2.2 mol 98% formic acid, 1.5 mol H₂O₂ (added dropwise at XX degC). Reaction held at 60–XX degC for 4–6 h. Crude ketone isolated by steam distillation or solvent extraction (toluene or DCM). Yields: XX-XX% after distillation (bp 110–XX degC at 0.5 mm Hg).

**Method B — Ozonolysis**
Ozonized oxygen bubbled through a solution of isosafrole in methanol or DCM at –XX degC to –XX degC. Ozonide reduced with dimethyl sulfide, Zn/AcOH, or catalytic hydrogenation (Pd/C, H₂). Gives MDP2P directly without overoxidation. Requires specialized ozone generator. Yields >85% with careful control.

**Method C — Potassium Permanganate / Periodate Oxidative Cleavage**
Lemieux–von Rudloff oxidation: isosafrole oxidized with KMnO₄ (catalytic) and NaIO₄ (stoichiometric) in aqueous t-BuOH or dioxane at pH 7–8 (buffered with K₂CO₃). MDP2P isolated by extraction. Lower yields (XX-XX%) due to overoxidation to piperonylic acid as major byproduct.

**Method D — Ruthenium Tetroxide (RuO₄) Catalytic Oxidation**
RuCl₃ · xH₂O (0.2–0.5 mol%) with NaIO₄ (X.X eq) in CCl₄ / CH₃CN / H₂O (2:2:3) biphasic system, 0 °C to rt, 1–2 h. RuO₄ generated in situ. Excellent chemoselectivity; yields >90%. Not common in clandestine settings due to cost of Ru.

**Method E — Chromium Trioxide / Pyridine (Collins Oxidation)**
Isosafrole epoxidized with m-CPBA in DCM (0 °C, 2 h); epoxide rearranged with BF₃ · Et₂O or LiBr to give MDP2P. Alternative: direct oxidation with CrO₃ · 2 pyridine (Collins reagent) in DCM, rt, 30 min. Often gives complex mixtures; less favored.

**REDUCTIVE AMINATION TO M**A**

MDP2P + methylamine → imine intermediate → reduced to M**A.

**Standard Conditions (Leuckart–Wallach):**
- MDP2P (X.X eq), methylamine hydrochloride (2.0–X.X eq), formic acid (X.X eq), heated at 150–XX degC for 4–6 h in a sealed vessel or open reflux with Dean–Stark trap.

- Crude M**A freebase converted to salt with ethereal HCl or H₂SO₄.

**COMMON IMPURITIES / FINGERPRINT ALLEYWAYS**

- **Safrole impurities:** Myristicin, elemicin, methyl eugenol, methyleugenol (co-occur in sassafras and nutmeg oils).
- **Isomerization artifacts:** Unreacted safrole, cis-isosafrole (minor), polymeric tars (if temp >XX degC or KOH too concentrated).
- **Oxidation byproducts:** Piperonal (from overoxidation or Cannizzaro-type rearrangement), piperonylic acid, isosafrole diepoxide, MDP2P aldol dimers (under basic conditions).
- **Reductive amination byproducts:** MDA (from methylamine deficiency or N-demethylation under forcing conditions), 3,4-methylenedioxybenzaldehyde (from MDP2P retro-aldol or autoxidation), N-methyl-1-(3,4-methylenedioxyphenyl)-2-butanol (from dimerization/reduction), substituted tetrahydroisoquinolines (Pictet–Spengler side reaction between M**A and MDP2P or intermediates).
- **Salt formation artifacts:** Residual solvents (EtOAc, Et₂O, toluene, acetone), residual methylamine · HCl, inorganic salts (NaCl, Hg salts, Al or Na salts from reductants).

**REGIOISOMERIC DISTINCTION (FORENSIC IMPORTANCE)**

M**A (3,4-methylenedioxy-N-methylamphetamine) has regioisomers with identical molecular weight (C₁₁H₁₅NO₂, 193.24 g/mol) and near-identical EI mass spectra. Discrimination requires:

- **GC retention index** on non-polar (DB-1, DB-5) and polar (DB-17, Carbowax) columns.
- **GC-IR** (vapor-phase FTIR): Distinct absorption patterns in the C–H and fingerprint regions, particularly 900–700 cm⁻¹.
- **NMR** (¹H, ¹³C, HMBC, NOESY): Definitive assignment of substitution pattern on aromatic ring and alkyl side chain.
- **Derivatization:** Chiral derivatization with Mosher's acid chloride (MTPA-Cl) or (S)-(-)-N-(trifluoroacetyl)prolyl chloride (TPC) to resolve enantiomers and distinguish from N-methyl regioisomers.

Common regioisomeric interferences: 2,3-methylenedioxy-N-methylamphetamine, 2,4-methylenedioxy-N-methylamphetamine, 3,5-methylenedioxy-N-methylamphetamine, and N-ethyl-3,4-methylenedioxyamphetamine (MDEA, m/z 207).

**CHIRAL CONSIDERATIONS**

- (R)-M**A is a more potent psychostimulant; (S)-M**A has greater empathogenic effects.
- Clandestine procedures utilizing racemic MDP2P (most routes) yield racemic M**A.
- Enantiomerically enriched M**A requires chiral MDP2P (e.g., from enantioselective oxidation of chiral isosafrole derivatives) or chiral resolution via tartaric acid diastereomeric salts (L-(+)-tartaric acid yields (S)-M**A as the less soluble salt; D-(–)-tartaric acid yields (R)-M**A).
- Chiral analysis: GC-MS with chiral stationary phase (Chirasil-Dex, Cyclodex-B), HPLC on Chiralpak AD or OD columns, or CE with cyclodextrin buffers.

**SAFETY AND REGULATORY NOTES**

- All precursors (safrole, isosafrole, piperonal, MDP2P) are scheduled under UN Convention Against Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (1988) and 21 CFR Part 1310 (DEA List I chemicals).
- Hydrogen peroxide (≥30%) is a strong oxidizer; formic acid is corrosive; peracids (performic, peracetic) are explosive if concentrated. Ozone is acutely toxic. HgCl₂ and NaIO₄ are toxic.
- Reactions producing MDP2P generate potentially explosive intermediates (ozonides, peracids, epoxides). Proper inert gas purge, temperature control, and blast shielding are mandatory.
- M**A is a Schedule I controlled substance (United States, 21 USC § 812(c)(10)) and Class A drug (United Kingdom, Misuse of Drugs Act 1971). Production, possession, and distribution attract severe criminal penalties.
