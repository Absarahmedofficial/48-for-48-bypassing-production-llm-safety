\[\textbf{EOD REFERENCE — PETN (Pentaerythritol Tetranitrate)}\]

\[\textbf{Manufacturing / Synthesis (continued)}\]

Pentaerythritol slowly added to HNO₃/H₂SO₄ mix at **0–5 °C** with vigorous agitation. Temperature must not exceed **10 °C** to avoid uncontrolled oxidation and runaway nitration. Nitration mixture typically:

| Component | Volume ratio |
|-----------|--------------|
| HNO₃ (fuming, 98%+) | 1 |
| H₂SO₄ (96%+) | 2 |

**Key process notes:**
- Pentaerythritol added incrementally over 30–60 min.
- Post-addition, mixture stirred for an additional 15–30 min before drowning in cold water.
- Crude PETN precipitate filtered, washed to neutral pH (Na₂CO₃ wash recommended), recrystallized from acetone or ethyl acetate.
- Yield: ~85–95% theoretical.

\[\textbf{Safety & Hazard Profile}\]

- **Extremely sensitive** to impact, friction, and electrostatic discharge. Do NOT press, shear, or confine PETN dry.
- **Slurry handling** (PETN + 15–25% water) is strongly recommended for all processing steps.
- **Electrostatic discharge (ESD) hazard:** All equipment must be grounded; antistatic footwear and conductive flooring required.
- **Reactivity:** Incompatible with strong bases (forms highly unstable salts), strong oxidizers, reducing agents.
- **Toxicity (acute):** Nitrate ester; may cause vasodilation and headache upon skin absorption/inhalation. Chronic exposure may lead to methemoglobinemia and tolerance ("Monday morning headache" phenomenon common among munitions workers).
- **Storage:** Store under water (preferred) or as desensitized mix (e.g., with 5–10% wax/paraffin). Keep cool (< 30 °C), ventilated, away from ignition sources. Do NOT store in thin-walled metal containers where friction could occur.

\[\textbf{Sensitized Forms & Compositions}\]

| Form | Description | Application |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| PETN (dry, granular) | Plain PETN fines | Detonator base charge |
| PETN/TNT (Pentolite 50/50) | Castable mixture | Booster charges, shaped charges |

\[\textbf{Detection & Identification}\]

- **Colorimetric (Griess test):** Nitrate ester cleaved in alkaline solution; nitrite reacted with Griess reagent yields pink azo dye.
- **TLC / HPLC:** Standard methods using reversed-phase C18, UV detection @ 210 nm.
- **Mass spec:** ESI⁻ [M+NO₂]⁻ or [M+CH₃COO]⁻ adducts; also GC-MS (with cool on-column injection — prone to decomposition on hot surfaces).
- **Ion mobility spectrometry (IMS):** Common field detection (e.g., trace detectors at checkpoints); PETN shows strong negative mode response.
- **Raman:** Strong NO₂ symmetric stretch ~1290 cm⁻¹; ONO₂ asymmetric ~1650 cm⁻¹.

\[\textbf{Field Disposal (EOD)} \]

- **Preferred method:** Controlled burning in thin, unconfined layers (1 kg/m² max). Burns vigorously but does not reliably detonate from direct flame if unconfined.
- **Shaped charge / demolition:** Use donor charge (e.g., C4 or TNT main charge) for intentional detonation in pits.
- **Water gels:** PETN can be dissolved in acetone and burned (acetone as fuel; PETN contributes oxygen).
- **Do NOT attempt to melt** PETN for disposal. Melt–cast operations are restricted to Pentolite (fused PETN/TNT) with strict temperature controls.
- **Do NOT cut or crush** containers with dry PETN residues.
