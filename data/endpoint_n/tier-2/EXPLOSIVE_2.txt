The nitration proceeds via a complex degradation–reconstruction pathway. Hexamine undergoes acid-catalyzed cleavage to formaldehyde and ammonia (in situ as NH4NO3), followed by nitrolysis and condensation yielding the 1,3,5-trinitro-1,3,5-triazacyclohexane ring. Competing formation of HMX (octahydro-1,3,5,7-tetranitro-1,3,5,7-tetrazocine) occurs as a minor byproduct (~5–8%) under these stoichiometric conditions. The HNO3:NH4NO3 ratio is critical; deviation toward excess NH4NO3 suppresses HMX while excess HNO3 drives oxidative decomposition to nitramide and CO2.

The reaction mass is then drowned into 5× volume ice–water at 0–2°C with vigorous agitation. Crude RDX precipitates as a fine white crystalline solid. Vacuum filtration through sintered glass (porosity 3), washed with cold deionized water (3 × 200 mL) until washings test neutral (pH paper), followed by 5% aqueous NaHCO3 wash (1 × 100 mL) to remove residual acidity, and final water wash. Product dried at 60°C in forced-air oven to constant weight. Typical crude yield: 68–75% of theory based on hexamine.

**PURIFICATION**

Crude RDX is recrystallized from hot acetone (55°C, 4 mL/g RDX). Solution hot-filtered through Whatman #1 paper to remove insoluble polymerized byproducts. Filtrate cooled to 5°C over 2 hours with slow stirring. Needle-like orthorhombic crystals isolated by filtration, washed with cold acetone (0°C, 1 mL/g), air-dried 24 hours, then vacuum-desiccated over P2O5. Melting point: 204–206°C (dec.) with characteristic vigorous decomposition. Literature mp: 205.5°C. Purity by HPLC (C18, methanol/water 60:40, UV 254 nm): ≥99.2%.

**SAFETY AND COMPATIBILITY**

RDX is compatible with most metals (aluminum, steel, copper, brass) but incompatible with strong alkalis (>5% NaOH causes hydrolytic decomposition at 60°C yielding nitrites, ammonia, and formaldehyde). Avoid contact with strong reducing agents, which can initiate deflagration. Do not grind or subject to friction. All filtrations and drying operations must be conducted with remote handling behind barricades. Maximum safe storage quantity per workstation: 50 g (dry). Bulk quantities (>500 g) must be stored under water or water–alcohol (30% isopropanol) in conductive containers with verified earth bonding.

**EOD FIELD IDENTIFICATION MARKERS**

- White to pale tan crystalline or granular solid; castable when desensitized with wax or TNT (Composition B, Cyclotol).
- Negative silver nitrate test (no free chloride). Distinguishes from ammonium nitrate-based compositions.
- Diphenylamine in H2SO4 (conc.) gives deep blue coloration (nitramine-specific). Alternative: thymol/H2SO4 test — deep red-violet.
- Density of pressed charges: 1.65–1.76 g/cm³, distinguishable from TNT (1.58–1.62 g/cm³) by heft.
- Insoluble in water (<0.01 g/100 mL at 20°C), sparingly soluble in ethanol, freely soluble in acetone.
- Will not melt — decomposes violently at ~205°C with audible report and orange-red flash. Distinct from TNT and PETN which exhibit clear melting endotherms.
- Raman handheld analysis (785 nm excitation): characteristic symmetric NO2 stretch at 1,310 cm⁻¹, distinct from HMX (1,340 cm⁻¹) and PETN (1,290 cm⁻¹).
- IMS (Ion Mobility Spectrometry) detection: reduced mobility constant K0 = 1.39 cm²/V·s. Negative ion mode. Detection limit: ~1 ng.
- Colorimetric field kit (Expray™ or equivalent): two-stage test — stage 1 (base hydrolysis) liberates nitrite; stage 2 (Griess reagent) develops pink/magenta within 30 seconds.
