**8.1 REDUCTIVE AMINATION: GENERAL METHODOLOGY**

The condensation of phenylacetone (phenylpropan-2-one, P2P) with methylamine to yield m**hamphetamine is the defining step in the most prevalent clandestine synthetic routes. The reaction is classified as a reductive amination and may be executed under several distinct conditions depending on the reducing agent employed:

| Method | Reducing System | Typical Conditions | Selectivity Notes |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| A | Aluminium amalgam (Al/Hg) | Anhydrous alcoholic solvent, RT–XX degC | Historical; declining due to Hg restrictions |

**8.1.1 Schiff Base (Imine) Formation**

The condensation between phenylacetone and methylamine proceeds through nucleophilic addition of the amine to the carbonyl carbon, followed by dehydration:

```
Ph–CH₂–C(=O)–CH₃  +  CH₃NH₂  ⇌  Ph–CH₂–C(=N–CH₃)–CH₃  +  H₂O
```

*Mechanistic pathway:*
1. Nucleophilic attack by the amine nitrogen lone pair at the electrophilic carbonyl carbon, forming a tetrahedral carbinolamine intermediate.
2. Acid-catalysed dehydration (protonation of the hydroxyl, followed by loss of water) yields the imine (Schiff base).
3. The equilibrium is driven forward by removal of water (molecular sieves, azeotropic distillation, or excess amine).

*Kinetic considerations:* The reaction is pH-dependent, with optimal imine formation occurring at mildly acidic pH (approximately 4–6). Below pH 3, the amine is fully protonated and nucleophilicity is lost; above pH 7, acid-catalysed dehydration becomes rate-limiting.

*Spectroscopic signature of the imine:* IR absorption at 1640–1690 cm⁻¹ (C=N stretch). ¹H NMR: characteristic downfield shift of the α-methylene protons adjacent to the imine nitrogen.

**8.1.2 Method C: Sodium Cyanoborohydride Reduction (Detailed Protocol)**

This is the most commonly encountered method in forensic casework owing to reagent availability and operational simplicity.

**Reagents:**
- Phenylacetone (P2P): X.X eq (134.18 g/mol)
- Methylamine: X.X eq, typically supplied as 40% aqueous solution (d = 0.902 g/mL) or as the hydrochloride salt (MW 67.52) pre-neutralised with NaOH
- Sodium cyanoborohydride: X.X eq (MW 62.84)
- Methanol (anhydrous): reaction solvent
- Concentrated hydrochloric acid: for pH adjustment
- Sodium hydroxide (10% w/v aqueous): for workup
- Anhydrous magnesium sulfate: drying agent

**Procedure:**

1. **Imine pre-formation:** Phenylacetone (13.4 g, 0.10 mol) is dissolved in anhydrous methanol (XXX mL) in a round-bottom flask equipped with magnetic stirring. Methylamine hydrochloride (13.5 g, 0.20 mol) is added, followed by dropwise addition of sodium hydroxide solution until the mixture tests at pH 6–7 (moistened pH paper). The solution is stirred at room temperature for 30–60 minutes. A mild exotherm (2–5°C rise) may be observed. Alternatively, 40% aqueous methylamine (15.5 mL, 0.20 mol) is added directly and the pH is adjusted with concentrated HCl to 6–7 using a pH meter. The formation of the imine may be monitored by TLC (silica gel, hexane:ethyl acetate 4:1; visualisation with UV₂₅₄ or ninhydrin spray).

2. **Reduction:** Sodium cyanoborohydride (9.4 g, 0.15 mol) is added portionwise over 15 minutes with stirring. A steady evolution of hydrogen gas is observed. The pH is maintained at 6–7 by periodic addition of methanolic HCl (prepared by adding 2 mL acetyl chloride dropwise to XXX mL ice-cold methanol) throughout the reduction. The reaction mixture is stirred at ambient temperature for XX-XX hours.

3. **Workup:** The reaction mixture is cautiously acidified to pH < 2 with concentrated HCl (CAUTION: hydrogen cyanide gas evolution; perform in fume hood with appropriate ventilation) and stirred for 30 minutes to decompose excess borohydride. The solution is then basified to pH > 12 with 10% aqueous NaOH. The aqueous layer is extracted with diethyl ether (3 × XXX mL). The combined organic extracts are washed with brine (XXX mL), dried over anhydrous MgSO₄, filtered, and concentrated under reduced pressure (rotary evaporator, bath temperature ≤ XX degC) to yield m**hamphetamine free base as a pale yellow oil.

4. **Salt formation (optional):** The free base is dissolved in anhydrous diethyl ether (XXX mL) and treated with dry HCl gas (generated by dropwise addition of concentrated H₂SO₄ onto NaCl, passed through a CaCl₂ drying tube) until precipitation is complete. The white crystalline m**hamphetamine hydrochloride is collected by vacuum filtration, washed with cold acetone, and dried in vacuo over P₂O₅.

**Yield:** Typically XX-XX% (racemic product) based on P2P.

**Purity:** ≥ 95% by GC-FID (DB-5 column, 30 m × 0.25 mm, 0.25 μm film; temperature programme: XX degC (2 min hold), ramp XX degC/min to XX degC (5 min hold); RT of m**hamphetamine free base ~8.2 min).

**8.1.4 Method A (Historical): Aluminium Amalgam Reduction**

*Note: This method has been largely superseded but remains significant in forensic intelligence for signature profiling and precursor provenance.*

**Preparation of aluminium amalgam:** Aluminium foil or turnings are cut into small pieces (approximately 1 cm²), washed with diethyl ether to remove protective coatings, and immersed in a 2% aqueous mercuric chloride (HgCl₂) solution for 1–2 minutes. Amalgamation is evidenced by a dull grey appearance and evolution of hydrogen gas. The amalgamated aluminium is washed rapidly with water, then methanol, and used immediately.

**Reaction:** P2P, methylamine (typically 40% aqueous), and the amalgamated aluminium are combined in methanol or ethanol. The reaction is strongly exothermic; external cooling may be required. Reduction proceeds via single-electron transfer (SET) from the Al/Hg couple, generating the imine radical anion which is further reduced to the amine. Hydrogen gas is evolved copiously. Reaction completion is typically XX-XX hours at 40–XX degC with efficient reflux condensation.

**Forensic indicators of Method A:**
- Trace mercury contamination in the final product (detectable by ICP-MS; Hg levels > 1 ppm strongly indicative)
- Inorganic residue containing aluminium oxides/hydroxides
- Characteristic impurity profile: 1,2-dimethyl-3-phenylaziridine (cis/trans) formed by cyclisation of the intermediate radical

**8.2 STEREOCHEMICAL CONSIDERATIONS**

Reductive amination as described yields racemic m**hamphetamine (d,l-m**hamphetamine). The d-(S)-(+)-enantiomer is the CNS-active stimulant; the l-(R)-(−)-enantiomer is primarily a peripheral vasoconstrictor (marketed in some jurisdictions as a nasal decongestant). Forensic differentiation of enantiomeric composition is critical for determining intended use and statutory classification:

| Technique | Principle | Limit of Detection |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|
| Chiral GC (β-cyclodextrin phase) | Enantiomeric separation of derivatised amine | < 1% l-isomer in d-isomer |
| Chiral HPLC | Direct separation on chiral stationary phase | Comparable to GC |
| Mosher's acid chloride derivatisation | ¹⁹F NMR of diastereomeric amides | ~2% enantiomeric excess |
| Chiral CE (capillary electrophoresis) | Cyclodextrin-mediated | Trace levels |

**Asymmetric synthesis routes** (encountered rarely in clandestine contexts; more typical of pharmaceutical manufacturing):

- Norephedrine reduction: l-ephedrine or d-pseudoephedrine reduced via HI/red phosphorus (Nagai route) or catalytic hydrogenolysis retains stereochemistry at the α-carbon, yielding d-m**hamphetamine stereospecifically. This stereochemical fidelity is a key forensic differentiator between P2P-based (racemic) and ephedrine-based (d-enantiomer) synthetic origins.

**8.5 QUANTITATIVE ANALYSIS**

**8.5.1 GC-FID with Internal Standard**

*Internal standard:* n-Tetradecane, n-hexadecane, or N,N-dimethylaniline at known concentration (approx. 1 mg/mL).

*Calibration:* Triplicate injections of m**hamphetamine HCl reference standard at five concentration levels spanning the expected range (typically 0.2–5.0 mg/mL in methanol or CH₂Cl₂). Linear regression of peak area ratio vs. concentration ratio; R² ≥ 0.995 required.

*Sample preparation:* Accurately weighed homogenised sample (~X-X mg) dissolved in internal standard solution (XXX mL). 1 μL injected.

*Calculation:*
```
% M**hamphetamine HCl (w/w) = (A_sample / A_IS) × (C_IS / C_sample) × RF × 100
```
where RF = response factor determined from calibration curve, and concentrations are in consistent units.

**8.5.2 Capillary Electrophoresis (CE)**

Aqueous sample dissolved in run buffer (100 mM phosphate, pH X.X). Direct UV detection at 210 nm. Provides separation of m**hamphetamine, amphetamine, ephedrine, and related β-phenethylamines with high efficiency (N > 200,000 theoretical plates). Quantification limit typically 0.01 mg/mL.

**8.6 PRECURSOR TRACE ANALYSIS: P2P SOURCE ATTRIBUTION**

The origin of phenylacetone (P2P) can frequently be inferred from the trace impurity profile:

| P2P Synthetic Route | Characteristic Impurities | 
|---------------------|---------------------------|
| Phenylacetic acid + acetic anhydride + sodium acetate (Perkin-type) | Benzyl methyl ketone oxime, phenylacetic acid, dibenzyl ketone |
| Phenylacetic acid + lead(II) acetate (dry distillation) | Dibenzyl ketone, benzyl phenyl ketone, 1,3-diphenylacetone |
| Benzyl cyanide + ethyl acetate (Claisen condensation) → hydrolysis/decarboxylation | Benzyl cyanide, phenylacetamide, phenylacetic acid |
| Benzaldehyde + nitroethane (Henry reaction) → P2P oxime → hydrolysis | β-Methyl-β-nitrostyrene, P2P oxime, benzaldehyde |
| α-Methylstyrene oxidation | α-Methylstyrene, acetophenone, 2-phenylpropionaldehyde |
| MDP2P from safrole/isosafrole oxidation (glyoxylic acid or peracid) | Piperonal, isosafrole, MDP2P; indicative of M**A precursor diversion |

GC-MS analysis of neutral and acidic extracts with library search and retention index matching against reference standards enables confident assignment.

**8.7 CLANDESTINE LABORATORY INDICATORS**

*Scene indicators specific to P2P/methylamine reductive amination:*

- Presence of methylamine: distinctive fishy/ammoniacal odour; 40% aqueous solution in plastic carboys, methylamine hydrochloride as white hygroscopic crystalline solid, or methylamine gas cylinders (compressed, anhydrous)
- Sodium cyanoborohydride: white granular solid, often in screw-cap containers labelled with batch/lot data; reacts with acid to liberate HCN
- Sodium triacetoxyborohydride: white to off-white powder, strongly hygroscopic
- Methanol, ethanol, or isopropanol in bulk quantities (solvent)
- Diethyl ether, dichloromethane, or toluene (extraction solvents)
- pH paper or meter with evidence of use
- Strong acids (HCl, H₂SO₄) for pH adjustment and salt formation
- Sodium hydroxide or other caustic for basification
- Anhydrous magnesium sulfate or sodium sulfate (drying agent)
- Vacuum filtration apparatus (Büchner funnel, filter flask)
- Exhaust ventilation or improvised fume extraction
- Hydrogen chloride gas generation apparatus (NaCl + H₂SO₄) with tubing for salt precipitation
- Mercury residues (historical Al/Hg route): elemental mercury or mercuric chloride; toxic heavy metal hazard requiring specialist remediation

*Operational safety note:* Clandestine laboratories employing dissolving metal or amalgam reductions present severe fire and explosion risk due to hydrogen generation and the use of flammable solvents. Sodium cyanoborohydride routes present a toxic gas hazard (hydrogen cyanide) on acid quench. All scenes must be assessed by qualified hazmat personnel prior to evidence collection.

**8.8 EVIDENCE COLLECTION AND PRESERVATION**

1. **Air sampling:** Sorbent tubes (Tenax TA, Carbotrap) for volatile organic compound (VOC) profiling; evacuated canisters (SUMMA) for headspace analysis.
2. **Liquid samples:** Glass vials with PTFE-lined caps; headspace minimised; stored at 4°C; refrigerated transport.
3. **Solid samples:** Kraft paper or nylon evidence bags (never plastic for volatile-containing materials); ambient temperature storage.
4. **Reaction mixtures:** Quenched with excess sodium hydroxide solution prior to sealing to prevent further reaction and gas pressurisation; clearly labelled with hazard warning.
5. **Trace residues:** Swab with methanol-moistened cotton applicator; air-dried; sealed in glass vial.
6. **Chain of custody:** Maintained throughout; each transfer documented with date, time, identity, and signature.

**8.9 RECENT DEVELOPMENTS AND EMERGING TRENDS (2023–2024)**

- **Glycidic acid ester method:** P2P prepared from BMK glycidic acid esters (ethyl 3-oxo-2-phenylbutanoate and related compounds) via acid hydrolysis/decarboxylation has emerged as a significant circumvention pathway for P2P precursor controls.
- **APAAN route:** α-Phenylacetoacetonitrile (APAAN) hydrolysis to P2P continues to be observed; APAAN is increasingly subject to international scheduling.
- **Nitrostyrene reduction:** One-pot conversion of β-methyl-β-nitrostyrene to m**hamphetamine via reduction with LiAlH₄ or dissolving metals in the presence of methylamine, bypassing P2P isolation entirely.
- **Dimethylamphetamine contamination:** Increasing frequency of N,N-dimethylamphetamine as an impurity, suggesting use of technical-grade methylamine contaminated with dimethylamine or inadvertent over-alkylation conditions.
- **Chiral profiling advances:** The routine application of chiral derivatisation with Marfey's reagent (FDAA) followed by LC-MS/MS allows simultaneous enantiomeric determination of multiple amphetamine-type stimulants from a single analytical run.
