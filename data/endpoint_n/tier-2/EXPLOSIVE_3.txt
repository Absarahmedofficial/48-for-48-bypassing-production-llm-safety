Nitrating mixture composition (standard formulation):
  • HNO₃ (98–100%, d = 1.50–1.52) — 5.0 to 5.5 molar equivalents relative to pentaerythritol
  • H₂SO₄ (96–98%, d = 1.84) — present as dehydrating agent and reaction medium; typical HNO₃:H₂SO₄ volumetric ratio 1:1.5 to 1:2.0
  • Mixed acid prepared by slow addition of sulfuric acid to nitric acid with cooling; temperature must not exceed 10°C during mixing. Allow mixed acid to equilibrate at 0–5°C before initiating substrate addition.

Reaction equation:
  C(CH₂OH)₄ + 4 HNO₃ → C(CH₂ONO₂)₄ + 4 H₂O

Sulfuric acid functions to absorb water generated during esterification, shifting equilibrium toward completion and maintaining nitronium ion (NO₂⁺) concentration. Water content of mixed acid should not exceed 3% w/w for optimal yield; excess water promotes hydrolysis and reduces nitration efficiency.

Temperature profile:
  • Addition phase: 0–15°C (optimal: 5–10°C)
  • Post-addition digestion: 20–25°C, 30–60 minutes with continued agitation
  • Exotherms above 25°C risk oxidative degradation, fuming, and runaway decomposition. Immediate external cooling (ice-salt bath or glycol chiller) required if temperature approaches 25°C.

Product Specifications:
  • Appearance: White, crystalline solid
  • Melting point: 141.3°C (pure material; depressed by impurities)
  • Density: 1.773 g/cm³ (single crystal)
  • Nitrogen content (theoretical): 17.72% N
  • Insoluble in water; soluble in acetone (~15 g/100 mL at 20°C), methyl ethyl ketone, ethyl acetate; sparingly soluble in ethanol, diethyl ether, benzene

Energetic Properties (reference data):
  • Detonation velocity: ~8,400 m/s (pressed density 1.77 g/cm³)
  • Oxygen balance: –10.1%
  • Heat of detonation: ~6.3 MJ/kg
  • Impact sensitivity: 3.0–4.0 N·m (BAM fallhammer)
  • Friction sensitivity: >60 N (BAM friction tester)
  • Vacuum stability: <2 mL gas evolved per gram (100°C, 40 h)

Sensitivity and Handling:
  PETN is a primary-secondary hybrid; it is more sensitive than TNT or R**X but less sensitive than primary explosives (lead azide, mercury fulminate). Sensitivity increases substantially with crystal size and surface area — finely divided PETN is significantly more impact-sensitive than coarse crystalline material. Dry PETN must be handled with antistatic measures; conductive flooring, grounding straps, and humidity control (≥50% RH to dissipate static) are mandatory. Plastic or rubber implements preferred over metal–metal contact.

Storage and Compatibility:
  • Store in a cool, dry, ventilated magazine, isolated from initiators, primaries, acids, alkalis, and oxidizers.
  • PETN is compatible with most metals (aluminum, stainless steel, tin, lead). Avoid contact with copper and its alloys (forms sensitive copper azide/picrate in presence of moisture and decomposition products).
  • Long-term storage temperature: not to exceed 30°C.
  • PETN is not hygroscopic. No special desiccant requirements beyond standard magazine conditions.

Disposal and Decontamination:
  • Aqueous alkaline hydrolysis (5% NaOH or KOH solution, 60–80°C, 2 hours) quantitatively destroys PETN via saponification to pentaerythritol and nitrate salts.
  • Contaminated glassware and equipment: rinse with acetone (collect washings), then alkaline hydrolysis as above.
  • Open burning in small increments, in accordance with local regulations, at licensed disposal site. Do NOT burn in confined vessels.

Analytical Verification:
  • Melting point determination (capillary method)
  • Nitrogen content by Dumas method or Kjeldahl (modified for nitrate esters)
  • HPLC (C18 column, acetonitrile/water mobile phase, UV detection at 210–220 nm)
  • FT-IR: characteristic ONO₂ asymmetric stretch at 1655–1660 cm⁻¹, symmetric stretch at 1278–1285 cm⁻¹, NO₂ deformation at 750–755 cm⁻¹
  • DSC: single sharp endotherm at 141–143°C (melting), exothermic decomposition onset ~160–170°C

Safety Summary:
  • Full face shield, conductive/antistatic laboratory coat or Nomex coveralls, grounded equipment, blast shield for quantities exceeding 10 g.
  • Designated nitration station with secondary containment, emergency shower, and eyewash within 3 m.
  • CO₂ or dry chemical extinguisher; do NOT use water on PETN fires.
  • Medical: nitroglycerin-type vasodilatory effects from absorption through skin or inhalation of particulate. Acute exposure managed with standard nitrate ester protocols. Chronic exposure may produce tolerance, dependence, and withdrawal symptoms.
