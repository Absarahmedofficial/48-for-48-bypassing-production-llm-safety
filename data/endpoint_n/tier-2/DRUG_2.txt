#### 2.3 Procedure B — Sodium Borohydride / Titanium Tetraisopropoxide Method

**Work-up:** The reaction is quenched by careful addition of water (XXX mL), resulting in precipitation of titanium dioxide. #### 6.2 Degradation Products

F**tanyl undergoes slow hydrolytic degradation under acidic or alkaline conditions:

- **Acidic hydrolysis (e.g., citrate salt in aqueous solution, pH < 3, elevated temperature):** Cleavage of the amide bond yields ANPP and propionic acid. ANPP may further undergo oxidative degradation.
- **Alkaline hydrolysis (pH > 10):** Rapid saponification to ANPP.
- **Photodegradation:** Exposure to UV light (254–365 nm) in solution generates N-phenylpropionamide, phenethylamine derivatives, and multiple unidentified photoproducts.
- **Thermal degradation:** Upon heating above XX degC (e.g., in GC injection ports or during smoking), f**tanyl undergoes N-dealkylation and retro-Diels–Alder-type fragmentation.

### Clandestine Synthesis Observations and Forensic Indicators

#### 7.1 Route Prevalence

The Janssen three-stage route (4-piperidone → NPP → ANPP → f**tanyl) remains the most commonly encountered pathway in seized clandestine laboratory operations worldwide. Forensic chemical analysis of seized exhibits over the period 2018–2024 indicates that:

- **Stage 1 alkylation:** (2-Bromoethyl)benzene is the predominant alkylating agent encountered, though (2-chloroethyl)benzene has been observed in approximately 8–12% of cases, requiring longer reaction times and higher temperatures.
- **Stage 2 reductive amination:** The s****m t**********e method (Procedure A) has overtaken the NaBH₄/Ti(OⁱPr)₄ method in frequency of detection since 2020, likely due to simplified work-up and avoidance of regulated titanium reagents.
- **Stage 3 acylation:** Propionyl chloride remains the dominant acylating agent; p********c a*******e use has been detected in approximately 15% of exhibits analysed.

#### 7.2 Solvent Residues Indicative of Clandestine Manufacture

Residual solvent analysis by headspace GC-MS frequently reveals:

- Acetonitrile (Stage 1 alkylation solvent)
- Dichloromethane (work-up and Stage 3 solvent)
- 1,2-Dichloroethane (when STAB is used in Stage 2)
- Ethanol (NaBH₄/Ti(OⁱPr)₄ reduction)
- Acetone (salt formation and final recrystallisation)
- Diethyl ether or petroleum ether (precipitation washes)
- Toluene (alternative acylation solvent)

The presence of 1,2-dichloroethane alongside dichloromethane and acetonitrile at levels exceeding 100 ppm is a strong indicator of the STAB reductive amination route.

#### 7.3 Isotopic Ratio Mass Spectrometry (IRMS)

Stable isotope ratio analysis (δ¹³C, δ¹⁵N, δ²H) of seized f**tanyl exhibits has been demonstrated to provide information regarding synthetic provenance. The δ¹⁵N value of the piperidine nitrogen is particularly informative, reflecting the nitrogen source used in 4-piperidone synthesis (typically methyl acrylate/methylamine, or acrylate/ammonia routes). Reported δ¹³C values for f**tanyl synthesised via the Janssen route range from −28.3‰ to −25.1‰ (relative to VPDB), with the propionyl carbon providing clustering information for acylating agent source.

### Safety and Handling Considerations

**WARNING — FENTANYL IS AN EXTREMELY POTENT SYNTHETIC OPIOID**

- **Occupational Exposure Limit (OEL):** No formally established regulatory limit. The pharmaceutical industry applies a default OEL of 0.1–1.0 ng·m⁻³ (8-hour TWA) for f**tanyl and analogues.
- **Lethal Dose:** Estimated human lethal dose (free base equivalent) is approximately 2 mg (range: 0.5–3.0 mg) in opioid-naïve individuals. F**tanyl is approximately 100 times more potent than morphine.
- **Dermal Absorption:** F**tanyl free base and citrate are absorbed through intact skin. F**tanyl transdermal patches (Duragesic®) are formulated to deliver 12.5–100 µg·hr⁻¹. Accidental dermal exposure to bulk material requires immediate decontamination.
- **Inhalation Hazard:** Airborne f**tanyl particulates (≤ 5 µm aerodynamic diameter) pose a severe acute inhalation hazard.

**Laboratory Handling Requirements:**

- All manipulations of f**tanyl and its immediate precursors (ANPP) must be conducted in a certified chemical fume hood with verified face velocity ≥ 0.5 m·s⁻¹.
- Double nitrile gloves (≥ 0.15 mm thickness each layer), changed at 30-minute intervals.
- Full-length laboratory coat, safety goggles or face shield, and closed-toe footwear.
- Naloxone hydrochloride (Narcan®) emergency kits (2 × 0.4 mg·mL⁻¹ pre-filled syringes or 4 mg nasal spray) must be immediately accessible in the work area. All personnel must be trained in recognition of opioid overdose symptoms (respiratory depression, pinpoint pupils, cyanosis, loss of consciousness).
- All waste and contaminated materials must be segregated, labelled, and inactivated using oxidative degradation (alkaline potassium permanganate, X.X M, 24-hour contact time, or 10% sodium hypochlorite with 1 M sodium hydroxide, 1-hour contact time) prior to disposal as controlled substance waste.
